# When Marriage Loses Its Boundaries: A Warning Sign of Civilizational Decline

*From a California Policy to Biblical Judgment and the Witness of the Church*

**Chongzhu Zhang (Paul Zhang)**

## Part One: A Policy in California—When the Definition of Family Begins to Change

When does a civilization begin to decline?

Is it when the economy falters, when a nation loses a war, or when its political institutions begin to fail?

History suggests that civilizational decline rarely happens overnight. A country may remain prosperous, its technology may advance, and its cities may shine with dazzling lights. Yet beneath that prosperity, the convictions that sustain society over generations may be quietly changing.

Marriage and family are among those convictions.

A family is not merely a place where two people share a home. It is also where a people passes on life, values, and responsibility to the next generation. If a nation is a magnificent building, its families are the stones upon which it rests.

### 1. An Apparently Ordinary Local Ordinance

In November 2026, West Hollywood, California, adopted a local ordinance concerning the registration of multi-partner domestic relationships. Under the arrangement, three or more qualifying adults may apply to register a voluntarily established multi-partner domestic partnership.

We must be precise about what this means. **A municipal domestic-partnership registry is not the same as California recognizing plural marriage, nor does registration automatically grant every right enjoyed by legally married spouses.** The measure concerns local recognition and certain practical protections; its exact legal effects depend on the ordinance.

Nevertheless, it raises an unavoidable question: What kinds of relationships should society officially recognize as domestic partnerships? What determines the boundaries of marriage and family? The order of creation? An inherited moral tradition? Or the voluntary agreement of adults?

### 2. From Protecting Individual Rights to Reconsidering Family

Supporters argue that people already live in a variety of relationships and may face practical difficulties involving visitation, housing, and everyday responsibilities. Government, they say, should provide appropriate protections. These concerns deserve serious consideration.

Yet **protecting a person's basic rights is not identical to redefining the public status of a relationship.** The first concerns fair treatment; the second concerns family identity, legal obligations, and the values communicated through public institutions.

Law does more than settle disputes. It also shapes how people understand life together. A change in a relationship registry may therefore become more than an administrative adjustment; it may open a broader debate about the nature of family.

### 3. As Freedom Expands, Does Responsibility Expand With It?

Freedom is a precious achievement of civilization. But freedom must travel with responsibility, just as rights must be accompanied by obligations.

Marriage means more than two people enjoying one another's company. It calls for fidelity, commitment, and responsibility through sickness, poverty, aging, and life's darkest seasons. It can also involve the upbringing of children, property, kinship, and responsibilities across generations.

When new forms of domestic relationships receive recognition, therefore, we must ask more than whether the adults involved consent. Who remains responsible when a relationship ends? How are vulnerable members protected when interests conflict? If children are involved, who safeguards their long-term well-being?

These questions do not deny anyone's human dignity. They recognize that family is more than a setting for adults to fulfill personal desires. **Family is also a community of responsibility.**

### 4. When Laws Change, Are Hearts Changing Too?

Some people understand marriage primarily through personal choice. Others believe it has a moral foundation beyond individual preference. Different worldviews produce different answers.

As a Christian, I believe marriage is not merely a social arrangement that humanity has developed over time. It also bears the relational, moral, and covenantal meaning given to human life by the Creator.

That belief does not authorize Christians to force others to accept their faith or to deny the dignity of people whose lives differ from their own. But a change in public opinion does not automatically overturn the church's convictions about marriage.

A society may offer more choices without producing deeper love. It may recognize more complicated relationships without necessarily building more stable families.

### 5. What Should Civilization Truly Fear?

A single municipal ordinance does not prove that American civilization has collapsed. Yet we should not ignore the ethical questions raised by institutional change.

Progress cannot be measured only by technology, wealth, or the number of available choices. We must also ask whether a society honors human dignity, protects the vulnerable, encourages faithfulness, and provides a stable environment for the next generation.

That leads to an ancient question: What happens when human beings no longer wish to accept moral limits beyond their own desires?

Scripture directs our attention to the days of Noah.

## Part Two: The Days of Noah—When Human Desire Rejects Restraint

Where does the moral collapse of an age begin? With changing institutions, or with hearts that gradually lose their reverence for God?

Civilizations seldom deteriorate in a single moment. People may slowly change their understanding of right and wrong, become accustomed to what they once rejected, and eventually stop recognizing the problem.

### 1. What Happened Before the Flood?

Genesis 6:1–2 describes the “sons of God” seeing that the “daughters of men” were beautiful and taking wives as they chose.

Christians have long debated the identity of the “sons of God.” Interpretations include descendants of Seth, angelic beings, and powerful rulers. We should not present a disputed interpretation as though no disagreement exists.

Even so, the passage invites a serious question: When desire, appearance, or power dominates a relationship, is marriage still understood as a covenant carrying lasting responsibility?

Beauty itself is not sinful. The deeper issue is whether desire submits to truth and whether those who possess power respect the lives of others.

**When desire becomes the master of power, and power becomes the servant of desire, a society's moral crisis may already be beginning.**

### 2. From Seeing to Taking

In Scripture, Eve saw the fruit, Achan saw valuable possessions, and David saw Bathsheba. Their circumstances were different, but each account warns that seeing something desirable is not the whole issue. The decisive question is whether desire crosses the boundaries established by God.

David used royal power to violate another man's marriage. Innocent people suffered, and his household endured lasting consequences.

**Sin is never merely a private event within the heart. Unrestrained desire can wound families, harm others, and damage the wider social order.**

People today often say, “It is my freedom.” But freedom is not the end of moral reasoning. We must still ask: Is this consistent with truth? Does it honor others? Does it accept responsibility? Does it build up human life?

### 3. The Greatest Danger Is Becoming Comfortable With Evil

Genesis 6:5 describes the great wickedness of humanity and the evil intentions of the human heart. Genesis 6:11 adds that the earth was corrupt and filled with violence.

The sin of Noah's generation, therefore, cannot be reduced to marriage or sexual ethics. Scripture explicitly identifies violence, oppression, and widespread corruption.

When people lose reverence for life, the strong may prey upon the weak. When power escapes restraint, desire may become the measure of everything.

A society's progress must be judged not merely by how many things people are permitted to do, but by whether they learn justice, self-control, faithfulness, and responsibility.

### 4. Life Continued as Usual, but Judgment Was Approaching

Jesus said that in the days before the flood people were eating, drinking, and marrying until the flood came (Matthew 24:38–39).

Eating and marrying are not sins. Jesus' warning concerns people who were absorbed in ordinary life and unprepared to heed God's warning.

They may have carefully planned a wedding without preparing their hearts to meet God. They may have organized tomorrow's affairs without considering their relationship with their Creator.

**Perhaps the most dangerous condition of an age is not that people openly declare their opposition to God, but that they have grown accustomed to living without Him.**

We cannot simply identify a modern nation with Noah's generation, nor can we claim to know the divine cause of every disaster. Yet Jesus' question remains urgent: Are we ready to stand before God?

### 5. The Flood and the Ark: Grace in the Midst of Judgment

Genesis 6:8 says that Noah found favor in the eyes of the Lord.

Scripture records not only the flood but also the ark. Judgment reveals God's righteousness; Noah's favor reveals His mercy.

Noah was not a man without weakness, yet he chose to walk with God in his own generation.

The account also challenges the church. We may criticize society's moral failures, but what credibility do we have if our marriages lack faithfulness, our families lack compassion, and our congregations are filled with rivalry?

**The flood tells us that God does not treat evil as inconsequential. The ark tells us that His judgment does not cancel His mercy.**

Human life continued after the flood, and cities rose again. But the human tendency toward sin did not disappear. Scripture next brings us to two prosperous but deeply corrupt cities: Sodom and Gomorrah.

## Part Three: The Days of Sodom—When Human Dignity Is Trampled

Does prosperity guarantee a healthy civilization? Can fertile land, wealth, and comfort tell us whether a society is morally sound?

The story of Sodom and Gomorrah forces us to confront these questions.

### 1. From Garden-Like Abundance to Profound Corruption

When Lot chose where to live, he saw the well-watered plain of the Jordan, described as being “like the garden of the Lord” (Genesis 13:10).

Yet Scripture immediately adds that the people of Sodom were exceedingly wicked before the Lord (Genesis 13:13).

Land may be fertile while hearts are barren. Cities may prosper while moral life deteriorates. People may be materially rich and spiritually impoverished.

Wealth can improve living conditions, but it cannot automatically improve character. Technology can increase efficiency, but it cannot automatically create conscience.

### 2. When Strangers Entered the City

Genesis 19 tells how two angels arrived in Sodom and received hospitality from Lot. That night, men of the city surrounded his house and demanded that the visitors be brought out to them (Genesis 19:5).

In context, this was a collective threat of sexual violence. No one asked whether the visitors consented. Their dignity and safety were treated as irrelevant.

**When a human body becomes an object for someone else's gratification, and violence replaces respect, the most basic moral foundations of civilization are violated.**

Traditional Christian teaching, drawing on Genesis 1–2, understands sexual intimacy as belonging within the covenant of marriage between a man and a woman. Jude 7 also speaks of sexual immorality and the pursuit of “strange flesh” in relation to Sodom, though interpreters debate the precise meaning of that phrase.

We should not equate the threatened gang assault in Genesis 19 with every contemporary same-sex relationship. Yet neither must the church abandon its understanding of biblical marriage. Fidelity to conviction and respect for human dignity must stand together.

### 3. Lot's Proposal: One Wrong Cannot Justify Another

Trying to protect his guests, Lot offered to hand over his two daughters (Genesis 19:8).

The suggestion is shocking. Hospitality can never justify sacrificing the safety and dignity of one's daughters.

**The Bible's description of an action does not mean the Bible approves of it.** Lot's proposal was not God's moral standard, nor can it be used to claim that sexual violence against women is somehow more acceptable than violence against men.

**We cannot defend a right principle by committing another wrong.**

If the church speaks of the sanctity of marriage, it must also confront adultery, domestic violence, sexual exploitation, and harm against children and vulnerable people. Defending marriage with words while failing to practice faithfulness leaves us with little more than an empty institution.

### 4. Sodom's Sins Included Pride and Indifference

Ezekiel 16:49–50 identifies additional sins of Sodom: pride, abundant food, careless ease, and failure to help the poor and needy.

Sodom's failure was not limited to things its people did. It also included the good they refused to do.

A materially prosperous society can still be morally poor if it loses compassion.

True civilization does not merely expand opportunity for the strong; it protects the weak. A serious family ethic asks not only who may enter a relationship but also who will accept responsibility for caring for others.

### 5. Collective Evil and the Warning of Judgment

The crowd outside Lot's home reveals the danger of collective moral disorder. When many people act together, individuals can become less willing to question their actions.

Yet numbers do not establish truth. A widespread social practice does not become good simply because it is common.

Genesis 19:24 records the destruction of Sodom and Gomorrah. But we cannot identify a modern disaster as God's punishment for a particular law. In Luke 13, Jesus warned against assuming that disaster victims were more sinful than others; He called everyone to repentance.

### 6. Before Judgment, Abraham Interceded

Genesis 18 describes Abraham pleading for Sodom. He asked whether the city might be spared for fifty righteous people, then continued until he asked about ten.

He did not deny the city's evil, nor did he rejoice at the thought of its destruction. He appealed to God's mercy while acknowledging God's justice.

This is a lesson the church must recover.

**Spiritual maturity is not merely the ability to recognize the sins of an age. It is the willingness to pray with tears for the people of that age.**

### 7. Sodom's Warning to Modern Civilization

Sodom possessed abundant resources but failed to build a just moral order. Its people enjoyed comfort but neglected the poor. Its city life did not prevent collective violence.

We cannot simply map this ancient account onto a modern city or municipal ordinance. Their legal and moral circumstances are fundamentally different.

Still, Scripture gives a lasting warning: whatever definition of family a society adopts, it cannot afford to abandon faithfulness, responsibility, justice, dignity, and protection of the vulnerable.

**If we condemn other people's sins while refusing to repent of our own, we have not truly heard the warning of Sodom.**

## Part Four: America Today—When Freedom and Responsibility Drift Apart

What is a nation's greatest achievement? Military strength? Technological innovation? Or institutions that protect human freedom?

America has drawn people from across the world: people seeking work, education, refuge, and opportunity. Having come to the United States from China, I know something of the preciousness of freedom.

Yet living here has led me to ask: **Does having more freedom necessarily mean possessing a more mature civilization?**

### 1. America's Greatness Includes Restraints on Power

The Declaration of Independence in 1776 affirmed equality and the rights to life, liberty, and the pursuit of happiness. These ideals were historically significant, even though early American society still contained slavery, unequal treatment of women, and other profound contradictions.

One of the important contributions of American constitutional government is its attempt to restrain abuses of power through law and institutional checks.

This reflects a serious understanding of human nature: people need freedom, but they can also misuse it.

The Christian worldview teaches that human beings bear God's image and therefore possess dignity. It also teaches that human beings are fallen and need the restraints of truth and justice.

**Freedom is not a license to do whatever one wishes. It is the space to pursue a good life while respecting others and accepting responsibility.**

### 2. From Covenant to Personal Choice

American views of marriage have changed over recent decades. Personal feeling, autonomy, and self-fulfillment have become more prominent.

Some developments have been beneficial. Marriage should not be an instrument of oppression or violence, and personal dignity must be protected.

But if immediate personal happiness becomes the sole reason for marriage, what sustains a promise when feelings fade, circumstances become difficult, and conflicts arise?

Marriage concerns not only love but faithfulness; not only happiness but sacrifice; not only choice but commitment.

### 3. Democrats and Republicans: Different Policy Emphases, Shared Ethical Questions

Progressive currents within the Democratic Party have generally emphasized personal autonomy, gender equality, and legal protections for diverse relationships. Social conservatives within the Republican Party have generally emphasized traditional marriage, parental rights, and religious liberty. Both parties contain internal differences.

In 2015, the U.S. Supreme Court recognized same-sex couples' right to marry in *Obergefell v. Hodges*. Congress enacted the Respect for Marriage Act in 2022. These were significant legal developments, but they did not establish nationwide recognition of plural marriage.

For Christians, a political party is not the church, and a party platform is not Scripture. No party automatically embodies God's righteousness.

The church must evaluate particular policies in light of biblical convictions while maintaining consistent standards regarding dishonesty, greed, violence, and abuse of power.

**When the church becomes the instrument of a political party, it risks losing the freedom to speak prophetically to the whole society.**

### 4. From Protecting Rights to Redefining Marriage

A local government's provision of relationship registration or practical protections is not identical to the church's recognition of a relationship as biblical marriage.

We can protect the safety and lawful interests of every person while maintaining the traditional Christian teaching on marriage.

The deeper question remains: If marriage becomes nothing more than a contract that adults may redesign at will, will its distinctive covenantal meaning gradually disappear?

### 5. Civilizational Regression Does Not Always Look Like Technological Backwardness

Civilization cannot be measured by technology alone. A society may possess artificial intelligence, immense wealth, and complex laws while still suffering from domestic violence, betrayal, loneliness, and indifference to the vulnerable.

What we need to preserve is not an idealized past, but the virtues of faithfulness, honesty, self-control, responsibility, respect, sacrifice, and love.

**If a civilization advances technologically while losing ground in responsibility, fidelity, and protection of the weak, its progress remains incomplete.**

### 6. What Scripture Means by Freedom

Galatians 5:13 teaches that believers are called to freedom, but must not use freedom as an opportunity for the flesh; instead, they are to serve one another through love.

Scripture affirms freedom but rejects the idea that freedom means self-indulgence.

Individualism may begin by asking, “What can this relationship give me?” Covenant love asks a further question: “How can I love this person faithfully and honor the commitment I have made?”

Ephesians 5 holds up Christ's self-giving love as a model for marriage. Such love must never be used to excuse abuse, coercion, or domination.

### 7. America's Challenge Is Not Only Legal but Spiritual

Law can define the conditions of marriage, but it cannot guarantee love. It can establish parental obligations, but it cannot automatically create patience. It can prohibit domestic violence, but legislation alone cannot remove selfishness from the heart.

Families therefore need sound institutions and renewed lives.

**A nation's renewal is not merely a transfer of political power. It also requires renewal of character, family responsibility, and moral life.**

### 8. Where Should the Church Stand?

The church must stand first on the truth of God.

That means refusing to abandon biblical convictions when public opinion changes. It also means refusing to excuse injustice within any political camp for the sake of advantage.

If the church believes marriage is sacred, it should begin by practicing fidelity in its own homes. If it believes family matters, it should spend time caring for families. If it believes every person bears God's image, it must respect those with whom it disagrees.

The church cannot remain at a distance merely issuing warnings. It must become a visible sign of hope.

## Part Five: What Kind of Witness Should the Church Offer?
### When Everything Around Us Changes, Truth Must Not Change

An age may change its laws, but it cannot change God.

A society may redefine marriage, but it cannot thereby change marriage's foundation in creation.

A nation may enact new rules through democratic procedures, but majority approval does not automatically make every choice consistent with God's will.

This is the conviction I cannot set aside as I write.

**For Christians, marriage is first a question of creation, God's authority, and our willingness to obey His Word.**

### 1. Marriage Is Not a Human Invention but Part of God's Created Order

Genesis 1:27 teaches that God created humanity in His image, male and female.

Genesis 2:24 describes a man leaving his parents, holding fast to his wife, and the two becoming one flesh.

According to the traditional Christian interpretation of Scripture, marriage is an exclusive, enduring covenant between one man and one woman.

The Old Testament records polygamy, concubinage, and complicated family arrangements. Recording them does not mean presenting them as God's ideal. Many of those narratives expose jealousy, conflict, and suffering.

When Jesus was questioned about divorce, He returned to creation. In Matthew 19:4–6, He referred to humanity being created male and female, to the two becoming one flesh, and declared that what God has joined together, human beings must not separate.

**The authority of marriage does not arise from human desire but from God's creation.**

This does not mean that someone suffering abuse must remain in danger. Protecting victims, stopping wrongdoing, and pursuing justice are responsibilities the church cannot abandon.

### 2. Laws May Change, but the Church Must Not Confuse Law With Truth

What the law permits and what Scripture approves are not the same thing.

Acts 5:29 declares that we must obey God rather than human beings.

This is not permission to disregard lawful authority or harm others. Scripture also teaches believers to respect legitimate government and fulfill civic responsibilities. Yet when human demands conflict with God's clear commands, the church must remain faithful to God.

In traditional Christian teaching, marriage is the covenant of one man and one woman, and sexual intimacy belongs within that covenant. Adultery, sexual immorality, and sexual exploitation contradict biblical holiness.

The church cannot automatically declare a relationship to be biblical marriage simply because a government provides for its registration.

**Truth must not be abandoned because people oppose it, and love must not be withdrawn because people are different.**

### 3. The Church's Greatest Danger Is Losing the Truth

Second Timothy 4:3 warns that people may reject sound teaching and seek teachers who tell them what they want to hear.

The church should understand the questions of younger generations, listen carefully to people's experiences, and explain its convictions with clarity and gentleness. But understanding an age does not require accepting every judgment that age makes.

Likewise, speaking harshly does not make someone more faithful to Scripture. Ephesians 4:15 calls us to speak the truth in love.

**The church may lose society's applause, but it must not lose the Word of God.**

It may face opposition, but opposition cannot become an excuse for losing compassion. The church will ultimately answer to Christ, not to the opinions of a particular age.

### 4. The Strongest Defense of Marriage Is to Live It

If the church teaches that marriage is a covenant between one man and one woman, do our own families embody covenant faithfulness?

If we oppose adultery, are we faithful in our marriages? If we stress family responsibility, do we make time for our spouses and children? If we ask society to honor marriage, do we care for those whose marriages are in crisis?

First Peter 4:17 reminds us that judgment begins with the household of God.

Ephesians 5:25 calls husbands to love their wives as Christ loved the church and gave Himself up for her.

A husband's calling is not to control his wife, but to reflect Christ's self-giving love. Marriage should be a place of mutual respect, shared responsibility, and growth in holiness.

**The church's most powerful defense of marriage is not the number of articles it publishes, but the number of families that truly embody the love of Christ.**

### 5. Truth Cannot Be Compromised, but the Door of the Gospel Must Remain Open

May people whose lifestyles differ from the church's teaching still come to church?

My answer is clear: Yes.

Romans 3:23 teaches that all have sinned and fall short of God's glory.

That includes everyone, pastors and church members alike. We are not saved because we are morally superior, but because of the grace of Jesus Christ.

Yet grace does not mean that God approves of everything we do. In John 8:11, Jesus told the woman caught in adultery that He did not condemn her and called her to leave her life of sin.

Jesus rejected humiliation while calling for repentance.

The church should care for people facing divorce, loneliness, and family trauma; call those involved in adultery to take responsibility; protect victims of domestic violence; and treat people wrestling with questions of sexual orientation, identity, and relationships with dignity, while honestly explaining its biblical convictions.

**The church must not cancel the call to repentance in order to welcome people, nor close the door of grace in order to emphasize repentance.**

This is not a compromise. It is the gospel revealed at the cross: sin is taken seriously, and sinners are not abandoned.

### 6. The Church Must Rebuild Family Discipleship

Deuteronomy 6:6–7 commands God's people to keep His words in their hearts and teach them diligently to their children.

Passing on the faith is not only the work of pastors or Sunday school teachers. It must happen at the dinner table, on the road, and in ordinary family life.

The church should help unmarried young people understand marriage, prepare engaged couples for communication and responsibility, support married couples through conflict, equip parents to raise children, and care for single, divorced, widowed, and traumatized believers.

Most importantly, marriage is not the ultimate purpose of life. Christ is.

**True church renewal is not merely an increase in attendance. It is seen in lives transformed by truth, families learning faithfulness, and young people becoming disciples of Jesus.**

### 7. In a Dark Age, the Church Must Be Light

Jesus said, “You are the light of the world” (Matthew 5:14).

Light does not exist merely to prove how terrible darkness is. It exists to illuminate.

The church should clearly teach that, according to its understanding of Scripture, marriage is a God-given, exclusive, enduring covenant between one man and one woman. That conviction does not change with municipal ordinances, party victories, or shifting public opinion.

But the church should also tell the world that whatever a person's past, that person may hear the gospel, seek help, and be treated with dignity.

**We do not need a church that changes its faith whenever the age changes. We need a church that faithfully bears witness to Christ in the midst of change.**

## Conclusion: The Foundation of Civilization Is Found Before God, Not in Human Hands

As I come to the end, I return to the question with which I began: When does a civilization begin to decline?

From a local policy in California, to the flood in Noah's day, to the destruction of Sodom and Gomorrah, we encounter recurring struggles of the human heart.

We possess freedom but resist restraint. We gain power without always gaining the wisdom to use it well. We seek happiness while neglecting the responsibility that sustains it. We create institutions, yet institutions alone cannot cure the sin within us.

This does not mean that every modern change in marriage law is equivalent to a biblical account of judgment. Nor do we have authority to predict when or how God will judge a particular nation.

But Scripture tells us that human beings are not the final masters of their own lives. The Creator has ultimate authority over life, marriage, and moral responsibility.

God sees the heart. He sees whether spouses remain faithful, whether parents care for their children, whether the powerful oppress the weak, whether the prosperous help the needy, and whether the church proclaims truth while living out Christ's love.

In Noah's day there was a flood, but there was also an ark. Sodom faced judgment, but Abraham still interceded. In the New Testament, the fullest revelation of God's justice and salvation is found in the cross of Jesus Christ.

The cross tells us that sin cannot be treated lightly. It also tells us that God has not abandoned the people He created.

Jesus said that heaven and earth would pass away, but His words would not pass away (Matthew 24:35).

This is the church's ultimate foundation: not a political victory or a perfect system, but God's enduring Word and the salvation accomplished by Jesus Christ.

**We do not need a truth that is constantly rewritten to fit the times.**

**We need people who faithfully obey God's Word in a changing world.**

**We do not need a church that does nothing but criticize the world.**

**We need a church courageous enough to proclaim truth, tender enough to weep for sinners, and willing to bear the cost of helping broken families.**

**We do not need merely to defend the legal form of marriage.**

**We need to embody fidelity, holiness, commitment, and love in our actual homes.**

I want to end with Joshua's declaration:

> “As for me and my house, we will serve the LORD.”
>
> —Joshua 24:15

This is not a decorative verse to hang on a wall. It is a confession to be lived throughout a lifetime.

When the times change, we will still revere God. When society debates, we will still hold fast to truth. When our families face hardship, we will still accept responsibility. When others choose a different path, we will still treat them with dignity and love.

For the true hope of civilization is not that humanity finally gains the power to redefine everything. It is that, even with freedom and power, we remain humble enough to confess:

**We are not God.**

**We need God.**

**Our families need God.**

**Our society needs God.**

**When marriage loses its boundaries, the church must not lose the truth.**

**When civilization faces a crisis, Christians must not lose their witness.**

**Even when heaven and earth pass away, we believe that God's Word endures forever.**
