# The Horses Are Running, but the Lamb Reigns --- An Unshakable Hope in a Shaken World

**Scripture: Revelation 6:1--8**

**Big Idea: Conquest, war, scarcity, and death are terrifying realities,
but they are not the final masters of history. The four horsemen can run
only within the limits permitted under the Lamb's sovereignty.
Therefore, the church must not fall into end-times panic. In a shaken
world, we reject the idol of power, practice peace and generosity, and
hold firmly to the hope of resurrection.**

## Introduction: Has This World Spun Out of Control?

Brothers and sisters, when we open the news today, we often see
disturbing things.

Wars continue. Nations threaten one another. Societies become
increasingly divided. Hostility grows between people. Prices rise, and
many families feel financial pressure. Disease, disasters, and death
continue to confront us.

Sometimes we cannot help asking:

"Has this world spun out of control?"\
"Where is God?"\
"If the Lamb has already conquered, why is there still so much
suffering?"\
"Are the four horsemen of Revelation already riding?"

Some people read Revelation 6 and immediately try to match every detail
with the latest headlines:

Which country is the white horse?\
Which war is the red horse?\
Which economic crisis is the black horse?\
Which epidemic is the pale horse?

But Revelation is not a codebook for guessing dates, nor was it given to
satisfy our curiosity about disasters.

John wrote Revelation to help pressured first-century churches see a
crucial reality:

**History on earth may be shaken, but the throne in heaven has not
moved.**

We must remember that Revelation 6 does not begin with the four
horsemen.

Chapter 4 first shows us the throne.\
Chapter 5 shows us the Lamb who was slain yet stands.\
Only then, in chapter 6, does the Lamb begin opening the seals of the
scroll.

The horses do not break down the door of history on their own. The Lamb
opens the seals, and then the living creatures call, "Come."

This is the central message of the sermon:

**The horses may run, but the Lamb opens the scroll. Disaster may rage,
but history remains under the sovereignty of the throne.**

## I. The White Horse and False Conquest: Counterfeit Victory Is Exposed Under the Lamb's True Authority (6:1--2)

John writes:

> "And I saw when the Lamb opened one of the seals, and I heard, as it
> were the noise of thunder, one of the four beasts saying, Come and
> see. And I saw, and behold a white horse: and he that sat on him had a
> bow; and a crown was given unto him: and he went forth conquering, and
> to conquer." (Rev. 6:1--2, KJV)

### 1. Observation: This Rider Looks Like a Victor

The first horse is white.

Its rider carries a bow, receives a crown, and goes out conquering. The
color white, the crown, and the language of conquest can easily remind
readers of Christ.

For that reason, Christians have interpreted this rider in different
ways. Some have understood him as representing the advance of the
gospel; others have seen conquest, false messianic power, or imperial
expansion.

Within the larger pattern of Revelation 6, however, the white horse
appears alongside the red, black, and pale horses in a sequence of
calamity. It is therefore more likely to portray the recurring power of
conquest in human history, including political, religious, or
ideological powers that can present themselves as saviors.

The point is not that every white image in Revelation is evil. The
warning is simpler:

**Not every power that looks victorious comes from the true Lamb.**

### 2. Interpretation: "A Crown Was Given unto Him"

The text says that a crown "was given" to the rider.

The Greek word is **στέφανος (stephanos)**, commonly used for a victor's
wreath or crown. The word itself is not negative; Revelation also uses
it for crowns received by God's people.

The important detail here is that this rider receives his crown. He does
not possess ultimate sovereignty in himself.

His authority is permitted.\
His activity is limited.\
His victories are temporary.

In Revelation 19, Christ Himself appears riding a white horse. He is
called Faithful and True, the Word of God, and King of Kings. On His
head are many crowns, using the word **διάδημα (diadēma)**, associated
with royal rule.

We should not mechanically turn the vocabulary into a rule that one kind
of crown is always false and the other always true. Yet Revelation
clearly creates a striking contrast between the scenes.

The rider of Revelation 6 receives a crown and goes out conquering.\
The Christ of Revelation 19 is Himself the King of Kings and possesses
ultimate royal authority.

Earthly conquerors possess authority only for a time. Eternal kingship
belongs to the Lamb.

### 3. Scripture Interprets Scripture: Counterfeit Victory Often Appears Before War

In Matthew 24, when Jesus speaks about the signs surrounding the end,
His first warning is:

> "Take heed that no man deceive you. For many shall come in my name,
> saying, I am Christ; and shall deceive many." (Matt. 24:4--5, KJV)

Only afterward does He speak about wars, famines, and earthquakes.

The sequence has a meaningful resemblance to Revelation 6:

first deception and conquest,\
then war,\
then famine,\
then death.

Evil does not always introduce itself with the face of the red, black,
or pale horse.

Sometimes it first rides in on a white horse.

It says, "Follow me, and I will make you safe."\
It says, "If only enough power is concentrated here, everything will be
better."\
It says, "If we conquer them, we will finally have peace."\
It says, "If you possess more, you will no longer be afraid."

Counterfeit conquest presents itself as salvation, but it cannot truly
save.

### 4. Contemporary Application: There May Be a White Horse in Our Own Hearts

Brothers and sisters, do not leave the white horse only in international
politics.

We may have a white horse in our own hearts.

We try to conquer our careers in order to prove our worth.\
We control our children so they will follow our plans.\
We manipulate a spouse so that person will meet our expectations.\
Even in church, we can pursue numbers, size, and influence as though
growth were the only proof of God's presence.

Effort, growth, and responsible management are not themselves wrong.

The question is whether we have turned success, expansion, and control
into saviors.

Why are we so desperate to control?

Because we do not feel secure.

We fear failure.\
We fear losing respect.\
We fear our children making mistakes.\
We fear being rejected.\
We fear circumstances slipping beyond our control.

So we climb onto the white horse and try to conquer our way into
security.

But the gospel tells us that the true Victor did not prove Himself by
conquering other people. He is the Lamb who willingly gave Himself on
the cross for sinners.

The cross tells you:

You do not need to control other people in order to prove your worth.\
You do not need career success to purchase an identity.\
You do not need to win every argument in order to be secure.

The Lamb has purchased you with His blood.

Your identity is not built on how much you have conquered, but on Christ
having given Himself for you.

### First Question

When things do not go according to your plans, how do you respond?

Do you raise your voice?\
Tighten your control?\
Punish people with silence?\
Use spiritual language to pressure them?

Or can you say:

"Lord, I trust that You reign. I can be faithful without trying to be
the savior."

The powers and success stories of this world are passing riders wearing
temporary crowns. Only the crucified Lamb possesses the eternal
authority of the King of Kings.

## II. The Red and Black Horses: War and Exploitation Rage, but Remain within the Limits of the Throne (6:3--6)

When the second seal is opened, John sees a red horse:

> "And there went out another horse that was red: and power was given to
> him that sat thereon to take peace from the earth, and that they
> should kill one another: and there was given unto him a great sword."
> (Rev. 6:4, KJV)

Then the third seal is opened:

> "And I beheld, and lo a black horse; and he that sat on him had a pair
> of balances in his hand." (Rev. 6:5, KJV)

A voice announces:

> "A measure of wheat for a penny, and three measures of barley for a
> penny; and see thou hurt not the oil and the wine." (Rev. 6:6, KJV)

### 1. The Red Horse: Peace Is Taken Away and People Kill One Another

Red naturally evokes blood.

The rider is given authority to take peace from the earth so that people
kill one another.

The text does not say that the rider personally kills everyone. Peace is
removed, and human beings turn against one another.

This reveals something terrible about sinful humanity.

When restraint gives way, hatred, jealousy, greed, and the desire for
power erupt.

James asks:

> "From whence come wars and fightings among you? come they not hence,
> even of your lusts that war in your members?" (James 4:1, KJV)

War does not begin only between nations. It also begins in the human
heart.

Cold wars inside families,\
factions within churches,\
hatred on the internet,\
hostility between peoples,\
and killing between nations

all reveal the same basic problem: sinful people want their own desires
to rule.

### 2. The Black Horse: War Is Followed by Scarcity and Injustice

The rider on the black horse holds a pair of balances.

Balances can represent fair measurement, but here they are used to
measure food at painfully high prices.

A denarius represented roughly a day's wage for an ordinary laborer in
the New Testament world. The announced prices therefore portray severe
scarcity: a day's labor can barely secure basic grain.

War damages production.\
Conquest disrupts order.\
Food becomes scarce.\
Prices rise.\
The vulnerable bear the burden first.

This is not the first time Scripture speaks about economic injustice.

The prophet Amos condemns those who trample the poor and exploit the
needy (see Amos 8:4--6).

God cares about worship, and He cares whether the poor can eat.\
God cares about prayer, and He cares about those who are crushed by
injustice.\
God cares about doctrine, and He cares how the weak are treated.

### 3. An Important Detail: The Voice Comes from among the Living Creatures

Revelation 6:6 says that John hears "a voice in the midst of the four
beasts."

The voice does not come from the rider of the black horse. It comes from
the direction of the throne, from among the living creatures surrounding
it.

This suggests that even while the black horse is running, the calamity
does not possess unlimited freedom.

"See thou hurt not the oil and the wine" may indicate that the judgment
remains limited. Some interpreters also see in the contrast a picture of
inequality amid economic crisis: staple grains become painfully
expensive while oil and wine remain available.

Whichever detail we emphasize, the clearest theological point remains:

The black horse cannot do whatever it wants.\
It does not establish its own boundaries.\
The throne still rules over the limits.

We should also avoid turning "hurt not the oil and the wine" into a
promise that believers will never experience financial loss during
economic crises. Revelation does not promise that the church will never
experience scarcity. It promises that disaster does not possess
unlimited sovereignty.

The black horse may raise the price of earthly bread, but it cannot
raise the price of heavenly grace. God's grace is still freely offered
to the thirsty.

Isaiah 55:1 says:

> "Ho, every one that thirsteth, come ye to the waters, and he that hath
> no money; come ye, buy, and eat." (KJV)

Earthly food may become expensive. God's grace is not reserved for the
wealthy.

### 4. Contemporary Application: In the Time of the Black Horse, the Church Should Become a Refuge of Grace

When inflation, unemployment, and economic pressure rise, our instinct
is usually anxiety and self-protection.

"Will I have enough?"\
"Will my investments lose value?"\
"Should I store more for myself?"\
"What happens to other people is not my concern."

But the church must do more than study the black horse. While the black
horse runs, the church must live out the kingdom.

Acts describes the early believers:

> "And all that believed were together, and had all things common; and
> sold their possessions and goods, and parted them to all men, as every
> man had need." (Acts 2:44--45, KJV)

This was not a coercive economic system. It was generosity produced by
the Spirit and by their identity as one family in Christ.

The church is not called to save the world through a political or
economic theory. We are called to make God's grace visible through
concrete love.

When someone loses a job, do we care?\
When a student has no transportation, do we help?\
When a new immigrant is struggling, do we walk alongside them?\
When medical costs place a family under pressure, are we willing to
share?

James asks what good it is to wish a needy brother or sister well while
refusing to provide what the body needs (see James 2:15--16).

In the time of the black horse, simply saying "Do not worry" is not
enough.

True faith opens our hands.

### The Church's Response

Against the hatred of the red horse, we live the gospel of peace.\
Against the greed exposed by the black horse, we practice generous
love.\
Against the isolation of crisis, we practice genuine fellowship.

We cannot stop every war in the world, and we cannot solve the global
economy by ourselves. But we can make sure that a suffering person who
enters the church knows that he or she has not been forgotten.

## III. The Pale Horse and the Threat of Death: Hades Follows, but Christ Holds the Keys (6:7--8)

John writes:

> "And I looked, and behold a pale horse: and his name that sat on him
> was Death, and Hell followed with him." (Rev. 6:8, KJV)

The rider is given authority over part of the earth through sword,
hunger, death, and the beasts of the earth.

### 1. Observation: This Horse Bears the Color of Death

The Greek word translated "pale" is **χλωρός (chlōros)**. It can refer
to a greenish color and elsewhere may describe green vegetation. Here,
in association with Death, it evokes the sickly color of a corpse.

This rider has a name: Death.

Hades follows behind.

The text names four forms of devastation: sword, hunger, death or
pestilence, and wild beasts. The language echoes Ezekiel 14:21 and its
four severe judgments.

Revelation is therefore not inventing horror imagery from nothing. It
draws on the language of Old Testament judgment to declare that a sinful
world will ultimately face the righteous judgment of God.

### 2. The Pale Horse Is Terrifying, but It Is Still Limited

The text again says that authority "was given" to them.

The authority does not originate in Death itself.

Second, the devastation is limited to a fourth part of the earth. That
is a severe judgment, but it is not total.

The judgment is real, yet bounded.

Disaster cannot exceed God's ultimate sovereignty.\
Death cannot seize final authority.\
Hades is not the master of the universe.

In Revelation 1, the risen Christ tells John:

> "I am he that liveth, and was dead; and, behold, I am alive for
> evermore, Amen; and have the keys of hell and of death." (Rev. 1:18,
> KJV)

Revelation 6 says that Hades follows Death, but Revelation 1 has already
told us where the keys are.

They are in the hands of Christ.

Death may knock at the door, but it cannot determine eternity.\
Hades may follow for a time, but it cannot finally imprison those who
belong to Christ.

By Revelation 20:14, Death and Hades themselves are cast into the lake
of fire.

Death is not the final victor.\
Death itself will be judged.

### 3. Scripture Interprets Scripture: The Horsemen and Jesus' Teaching about the End

In Matthew 24, Jesus speaks about false christs, wars and rumors of
wars, famines, earthquakes, and persecution.

These themes clearly resonate with Revelation 6.

Yet Jesus says:

> "For all these things must come to pass, but the end is not yet."
> (Matt. 24:6, KJV)

And:

> "All these are the beginning of sorrows." (Matt. 24:8, KJV)

Therefore, when the church sees war, disease, and disaster, we should
not immediately announce that a particular date is the end of the world.

Jesus was not trying to satisfy the disciples' curiosity about dates. He
was calling them:

Do not be deceived.\
Do not panic.\
Endure faithfully.\
Continue bearing witness to the gospel.

Revelation 6 works in a similar way.

The four horsemen are not given to manufacture end-times anxiety. They
awaken the church to reality:

This world is not eternal.\
Sin produces real consequences.\
Judgment is real.\
Only the Lamb is a trustworthy refuge.

### 4. Pastoral Clarification: Will God's People Experience These Sufferings?

Someone may ask:

"Does this mean the four horsemen will never touch Christians?"

Revelation does not give believers a simple promise of exemption from
war, hunger, disease, and death.

The churches of Revelation 2--3 are already suffering.\
Under the fifth seal, servants of God have been killed because of their
witness.\
In Revelation 7, the multitude in white robes comes out of great
tribulation.

We should therefore not say that the horsemen can never affect God's
children.

Scripture gives us a deeper promise:

Disaster may touch our bodies, but it cannot erase our identity as those
who belong to God.\
Death may end our present earthly life, but it cannot separate us from
the love of Christ.\
God's seal is not a simplistic guarantee that we will never suffer; it
is the assurance that even in suffering we belong to Him.

Revelation 7 will further show God knowing, preserving, and sealing His
servants.

Romans 8 asks:

> "Who shall separate us from the love of Christ? shall tribulation, or
> distress, or persecution, or famine, or nakedness, or peril, or
> sword?" (Rom. 8:35, KJV)

Paul does not say that these things will never happen to believers.

He says that even when they happen, they cannot separate us from the
love of Christ.

### 5. Contemporary Application: How Should We Face Death and End-Times Anxiety?

Brothers and sisters, some people become more frightened the more they
read Revelation.

They fear war.\
They fear viruses.\
They fear economic collapse.\
They fear for their children's future.\
They fear that they may not escape disaster.

But Christian peace is not built on the promise that nothing bad will
ever happen.

Our peace is built on this:

Even when terrible things happen, the Lamb still reigns.\
Even when I walk through the valley of the shadow of death, the Lord
remains with me.\
Even if my body dies, I will stand in the resurrection.

Hebrews 2:14--15 teaches that through death Christ destroyed the one who
had the power of death and delivered those who through fear of death
were subject to lifelong bondage.

Jesus does not stand far away from death and simply tell us not to be
afraid.

He entered death Himself.\
He came out of the grave.\
He holds the keys of Death and Hades.

Therefore, we do not treat death lightly, but neither do we recognize it
as our final ruler.

When someone we love becomes ill, we may weep.\
When death approaches, we may feel fear.\
When the world is filled with disaster, we may grieve.

But our grief is not without hope.

Paul cries:

> "O death, where is thy sting? O grave, where is thy victory?" (1 Cor.
> 15:55, KJV)

The pale horse is still running.

But the empty tomb already exists.

## Conclusion: Has the World Spun Out of Control?

Brothers and sisters, let us return to our opening question:

"Has this world spun out of control?"

Revelation 6 answers:

**No.**

Conquest is real.\
War is real.\
Rising prices and economic injustice are real.\
Disease, disaster, and death are real.

Scripture never asks us to pretend that these things do not exist.

But more real and more enduring than all these calamities is this fact:

**The Lamb has conquered.**

The four horsemen do not simply burst into history by themselves. They
move only after the seals are opened and the command is given.

Their authority is given.\
Their scope is limited.\
Their time is finite.

They may devastate, but they cannot reign.\
They may shake the world, but they cannot shake the throne of God.

**The horses are running, but the Lamb reigns.**

Earthly powers may ride a white horse in apparent triumph, but the true
King is the Lamb who was crucified.

The red horse may remove earthly peace, but Christ has made peace
through the cross.

The black horse may bring scarcity and injustice, but God's grace is
still freely given, and His church is called to share with one another.

The pale horse may bring death near, but the keys of Death and Hades
remain in the hands of the risen Lord.

Therefore, the church must not become a panicked army in retreat or a
group of indifferent spectators.

By the grace of the Lamb:

Against the greed of the black horse, we practice self-giving love.\
Against the hatred of the red horse, we proclaim and practice the gospel
of peace.\
Against the shadow of the pale horse, we hold to resurrection hope.\
Against the counterfeit conquest of the white horse, we worship Christ
alone.

The sound of hooves belongs to an old world being shaken.

The unmoved throne is the assurance that the new heaven and new earth
will surely come.

Hebrews 12:28 says:

> "Wherefore we receiving a kingdom which cannot be moved, let us have
> grace, whereby we may serve God acceptably with reverence and godly
> fear." (KJV)

The world will shake.\
Economies will shake.\
Nations will shake.\
Our bodies will shake.

But we have received a kingdom that cannot be shaken.

## Response and Practice

Today, consider three questions before the Lord:

First, am I riding my own white horse, seeking security through success,
control, or influence?

Second, while the red and black horses run, am I concerned only with
protecting myself, or am I willing to become an instrument of peace,
generosity, and mercy?

Third, when I face sickness, death, and uncertainty about the future, do
I keep staring at the pale horse, or do I fix my eyes on Christ, who
holds the keys of Death and Hades?

This week, practice three concrete things:

Lay down one situation you have been trying to control and entrust it to
the Lord.\
Help one person who is experiencing financial or practical pressure.\
Use Revelation 1:18 and Romans 8:35--39 to pray for yourself or for
someone who is afraid.

## Closing Prayer

Father God, seated upon the throne, we worship You.

Lord Jesus Christ, You are the Lamb who was slain and yet stands. You
are worthy to open the scroll, and You govern the course of history.

We confess that when the world is shaken, we are often afraid. When
things do not follow our plans, we try to control. When resources become
tight, we become selfish. When the shadow of death comes near, we lose
hope.

Forgive us.

Lord, help us not to hear only the hoofbeats of the four horses and
forget to look up at the Lamb upon the throne.

When the white horse tempts us with counterfeit success, let us find our
identity and security in the cross.

When the red horse brings hatred and conflict, make us peacemakers who
confront hostility with the gospel of peace.

When the black horse brings scarcity and injustice, open our hands and
make the church a household of grace that shares with one another and
supports the weak.

When the pale horse and death draw near, remind us that You hold the
keys of Death and Hades. Neither tribulation, distress, famine, danger,
nor sword can separate us from Your love.

May we live in this shaken age without panic, indifference, or
compromise, bearing faithful witness by the grace of the Lamb.

The horses are running, but the Lamb reigns.\
The world will be shaken, but Your kingdom cannot be shaken.

In the victorious name of Jesus Christ we pray. Amen.
