> **Revelation 11:3–6 (KJV)**
>
> 3 And I will give power unto my two witnesses, and they shall prophesy a thousand two hundred and threescore days, clothed in sackcloth.
>
> 4 These are the two olive trees, and the two candlesticks standing before the God of the earth.
>
> 5 And if any man will hurt them, fire proceedeth out of their mouth, and devoureth their enemies: and if any man will hurt them, he must in this manner be killed.
>
> 6 These have power to shut heaven, that it rain not in the days of their prophecy: and have power over waters to turn them to blood, and to smite the earth with all plagues, as often as they will.

## Introduction: When the World Does Not Want to Hear the Truth

Brothers and sisters, peace to you in the Lord! Today I want to ask you a question.

If someone came among us to proclaim God's Word without fine clothes or an impressive appearance, and his words made us uncomfortable, would we still be willing to listen? Do we judge a witness by outward respectability, or by faithfulness to God's Word?

If one day speaking the truth came at a cost, would you still be willing to speak?

If honesty meant losing a business deal, if holding to biblical principles meant losing friends, or if openly acknowledging that you are a Christian meant losing certain opportunities, would you still follow the Lord?

What does the church most lack today?

A beautiful building? Abundant offerings? Advanced equipment?

These things certainly have their value. But I am concerned that what the church most lacks today is people who still dare to bear witness to Christ in the darkness.

Some people dare not acknowledge that they are Christians in business, fearing a loss of profit. In their relationships, they dare not hold to the truth, fearing offense. In the face of evil, they dare not speak, fearing the loss of those relationships.

We can sing loudly inside the church and yet remain silent before the world.

Brothers and sisters, if our faith can remain only within the sanctuary, and we dare not live it once we leave, whom are we really following?

In Revelation 11, God shows John two witnesses.

They do not wear splendid clothes. They do not come with worldly glory or rely on powerful political forces.

They wear sackcloth.

In a world that resists the truth, they faithfully proclaim God's message.

God has not promised that His witnesses will always be welcomed, but He gives them the authority they need to fulfill their mission.

Today, let us consider three important truths from these four verses.

## 1. Clothed in Sackcloth — The World Needs Repentance, Not Religious Performance

Scripture: Revelation 11:3

> “And I will give power unto my two witnesses, and they shall prophesy a thousand two hundred and threescore days, clothed in sackcloth.” (Revelation 11:3, KJV)

### 1. Why Two Witnesses?

Under Old Testament law, the testimony of two witnesses had significant legal force.

Deuteronomy 19:15 teaches that a matter is established by the testimony of two or three witnesses.

Thus, the vision of two witnesses carries the significance of solemn proclamation and testimony to the truth.

As to whether they are two particular prophets at the end of the age or symbolize the prophetic witness of God's people, different interpretive traditions understand them differently.

We need not be overly dogmatic where the passage does not fully explain. But one thing is very clear: they have not come out to seek a platform for themselves. God Himself sends them.

God speaks of “my two witnesses.” (Revelation 11:3, KJV, excerpt)

A witness's identity begins with God's calling. A witness's message must also remain faithful to God's Word.

In our church, brothers and sisters preparing for baptism write their testimonies of receiving God's grace after completing introductory instruction in the faith: why they believe in Jesus, how they have come to know Him, and why they wish to be baptized. If someone goes on to prepare for theological study and preaching ministry, that person also needs to examine the testimony of their calling: why they are taking this path of service, and how they have received God's call. This is a response to God's trust, rather than a search for a platform on which to be noticed.

When I taught students in theological training in China, I often reminded them at the outset: if you are going to be a preacher, prepare your heart. When you stand to speak on God's behalf, people will sometimes dislike what they hear. Faithful service to the Lord requires a willingness to pay a price.

Brothers and sisters, a preacher's most important responsibility is to proclaim faithfully the truth God has revealed, rather than to make everyone like him.

### 2. Why Are They Wearing Sackcloth?

The Chinese Union Version's term here refers to the coarse clothing worn in mourning and repentance, commonly called sackcloth. It does not mean a modern knitted sweater.

In Old Testament times, sackcloth was often associated with sorrow, humility, and repentance.

When the people of Nineveh heard Jonah proclaim judgment, they put on sackcloth as an expression of repentance (see Jonah 3:4–8).

The two witnesses clothed in sackcloth also remind us of the burden carried by Old Testament prophets as they confronted sin.

They have not come to display their own power or to celebrate the world's prosperity. In a rebellious generation, they bear a solemn mission of witness.

Brothers and sisters, true prophetic witness grieves over sin and calls people back to God. It does not stand before sinners to display its own holiness.

The church today needs to think again.

Are we willing to speak only of blessing and unwilling to speak of repentance? Only of grace and unwilling to speak of God's holiness? Do we want only to make people happy, without asking them to face their sin?

If a sermon never leads people to examine their lives, but only makes them feel good about themselves, we need to ask whether it has proclaimed the full message of Scripture.

### 3. What Are We Most Unwilling to Repent Of Today?

In the past, some people made counterfeit or inferior products to earn money. Today, business methods have changed, but human greed has not.

Some run cross-border online businesses and knowingly exaggerate their advertising with misleading product descriptions.

Some circulate unverified information in investment groups simply because they may profit from it.

Some become absorbed in stocks, crypto assets, or other speculative trading. They watch prices all day, their emotions rising and falling with the figures in their accounts, while spending less and less time praying, reading Scripture, or caring for their families.

Investing is not necessarily sinful. Running a business and seeking a reasonable profit are not sins either.

But when money begins to rule our conscience, when profit becomes life's highest goal, money has taken a place that does not belong to it.

The Lord Jesus says:

> “Ye cannot serve God and mammon.” (Matthew 6:24, KJV, excerpt)

Brothers and sisters, the real issue is whether money has become your master, whatever the amount you possess.

Some people's bank accounts grow richer while their souls grow poorer. Some become more successful in their careers while family relationships grow colder. Some gladly give an offering on Sunday but refuse to correct their dishonesty during the week.

Brothers and sisters, do not suppose that God sees only how we appear at church. He also sees our accounts, transactions, words, and hidden motives.

Repentance is more than shedding a few tears on Sunday and continuing the same life on Monday. Repentance means acknowledging our sin, leaving unrighteous ways, and accepting Christ's rule over our lives.

If we profit through deception, we should stop deceiving and do what we can to repair the harm we have caused.

If greed has led us to neglect our families, we should take up again our responsibility to love and care for them.

If we lie to save face, we should learn to admit our mistakes.

These actions do not purchase salvation. They are the fruit that those who have received grace should bear.

Brothers and sisters, sackcloth reminds us that the world needs more than successful people. It needs people willing to repent before God.

## 2. Standing Before the Lord — Witness Depends on the Holy Spirit, Not Worldly Power

Scripture: Revelation 11:4

> “These are the two olive trees, and the two candlesticks standing before the God of the earth.” (Revelation 11:4, KJV)

### 1. The Two Olive Trees and the Golden Lampstand

This verse takes us back to Zechariah 4.

When the Israelites returned from exile, they faced the difficult task of rebuilding the temple. Their strength was limited, their resources insufficient, and they faced opposition from outside.

Through the prophet Zechariah, God declared to Zerubbabel:

> “Not by might, nor by power, but by my spirit, saith the LORD of hosts.” (Zechariah 4:6, KJV, excerpt)

Brothers and sisters, this is an important background for understanding Revelation 11:4.

The vision of the olive trees and lampstand shows us the connection between God's provision and the mission of witness.

Revelation 1 has already used lampstands to symbolize churches (see Revelation 1:20). Their appearance again in chapter 11 also brings to mind the witness of God's people in the world.

However we understand the specific identity of the two witnesses, verse 4 particularly emphasizes that they stand before the Lord of the earth.

The Lord of the earth is neither the Roman emperor nor an earthly power. He is the God who rules heaven and earth.

They are able to stand because God sends them to stand, rather than because the world permits them to do so.

### 2. What Are We Really Relying On?

Brothers and sisters, the church today has increasing resources: buildings, equipment, funds, and online platforms.

All these can be tools for serving God. But tools cannot take the place of the Holy Spirit.

A church can have the most advanced sound system and yet lack true worship. It can have the most beautiful website and yet lack a burden for lost souls. It can receive enormous offerings and yet lack a life of mutual love.

Brothers and sisters, the church's greatest danger is that after obtaining resources, it gradually forgets its need for God.

In the past, some older preachers in my hometown of Wenzhou lived under difficult conditions. They may not have received systematic theological training, nor did they have all the resources available to us today.

We should not romanticize poverty or treat a lack of training as a spiritual virtue. But among them were people whose zeal for the gospel and faithfulness in serving through hardship are worthy of our learning.

Some time ago, a brother from a church I previously pastored in China told me that its building had been renovated and sent me photographs. Seeing it renewed inside and out, I also found myself reflecting: what are we really relying on? A church can make good use of buildings and equipment, but outward splendor cannot replace a burden for souls or a life of mutual love.

Today, with better conditions, we should make good use of them. Yet we must not lose our dependence on the Lord simply because we have better tools.

### 3. The Holy Spirit's Power First Makes Us Faithful to Christ

Some say that olives must be pressed to produce oil so that the lampstand can shine.

We may use this as an illustration from everyday life to remind ourselves that faithfulness sometimes comes at a cost. But we cannot treat it as the direct interpretation of Zechariah 4. Nor can we say that people must first suffer enough to obtain the Holy Spirit's power.

The Holy Spirit is God's gracious gift, rather than a reward earned through pain.

The real emphasis is this: the Holy Spirit enables us to depend on God in weakness, hold to the truth in temptation, and continue bearing witness to Christ in difficulty.

The Spirit's power does more than make our speech forceful.

It makes a proud person willing to be humble, a greedy person willing to be content, a habitual deceiver willing to be honest, and someone afraid of losing profit willing to pay a price for the truth.

Brothers and sisters, sometimes we imagine that the greatest miracle is moving a mountain. Yet in everyday life, someone long enslaved by money who becomes willing to repent through Christ's grace also displays God's mighty power to change a life.

Remember: true spiritual power makes us stand faithfully before God; it does not put us above other people.

## 3. Fire from Their Mouths — God's Justice Must Not Be Despised, and Witness Must Not Be Silenced

Scripture: Revelation 11:5–6

> “And if any man will hurt them, fire proceedeth out of their mouth, and devoureth their enemies” (Revelation 11:5, KJV, excerpt).

The passage goes on to say that they have authority to shut heaven so that no rain falls, to turn water into blood, and to strike the earth with various plagues.

Brothers and sisters, we must not pass lightly over these two verses.

They speak of more than the courage of the witnesses. They declare that God is the holy and righteous Lord. A rebellious world cannot despise His authority forever.

### 1. Fire and Plagues: God's Judgment Is No Empty Word

The vision reminds us of two important Old Testament prophets.

Elijah proclaimed drought at God's command. Moses, sent by God, performed signs in Egypt and turned water into blood.

Revelation uses these Old Testament prophetic images to show the solemn authority of the witness God commissions.

Different interpretive traditions disagree about how to understand the fire, drought, and blood described here. But together they point to an important truth: everything in heaven and earth is under God's sovereignty, and God has the right to judge the evil that resists His will.

Today, many people are willing to believe in a loving God but unwilling to believe that He is also a righteous Judge.

Some say, “As long as God loves me, it does not matter how I live.” Some think, “As long as nobody finds out, what I do is no problem.” Others mistake God's patience for His approval of sin.

Brothers and sisters, Scripture has never taught this.

God is loving, and He is holy. He delights to forgive repentant sinners, but He will never treat evil as unimportant.

Revelation 9 has already recorded how people suffered calamities and still refused to repent. This reveals the hardness of the human heart.

The terrible thing is more than the coming of God's judgment: it is that people still refuse to turn back when confronted with His warning.

### 2. God's Justice Is Not a Weapon for a Preacher's Revenge

Brothers and sisters, we must distinguish carefully.

These two witnesses possess a special prophetic authority. This does not mean that every preacher today can pronounce calamity on others at will. Still less does it mean that we may take revenge in God's name on those who oppose us.

The Lord Jesus teaches His disciples to love their enemies and pray for those who persecute them (see Matthew 5:44).

The authority to judge belongs to God. We must not dress up our own anger as God's wrath.

Yet neither should fear of misusing the message of judgment make us completely afraid to proclaim God's justice.

A truly faithful preacher must neither use judgment to frighten people for personal satisfaction nor conceal God's warnings against sin to please people.

We should speak of God's judgment with reverence, and pray with tears for those who have not yet repented.

### 3. One Thousand Two Hundred and Sixty Days: God Appoints the Time for Witness

Verse 3 tells us that the two witnesses prophesy for 1,260 days. This corresponds to the forty-two months mentioned earlier.

Different interpretive traditions understand this period differently: some as an actual period at the end of the age, others as a symbol of a limited time of tribulation.

But the vision clearly shows that God governs both the mission of witness and the course of history.

Brothers and sisters, we must not suppose that God's plan has failed because the world opposes it. Nor should we think that evangelism is meaningless because we do not immediately see results.

The next passage tells us that after the two witnesses finish their testimony, they will be killed (see Revelation 11:7).

This reminds us that the authority God gives does not guarantee that His servants will never suffer. It shows that the world's hostility cannot overturn His mission.

The world may reject the truth. People may ridicule faith. Some may compromise for profit. Yet God's church is still called to faithful witness.

Brothers and sisters, what most calls for watchfulness today is this: have we already voluntarily abandoned our witness even when no one is persecuting us?

For the sake of business, we are unwilling to acknowledge the truth. For the sake of relationships, we refuse to stand by principle. For the sake of a comfortable life, we avoid the responsibility of service.

We are not always forced into silence by the world. Sometimes we choose silence ourselves.

Brothers and sisters, do not let money buy our conscience. Do not let comfort rob us of our mission. Do not let the world's applause replace God's call to faithfulness.

## The Gospel's Climax: Jesus Christ, the Faithful Witness

Brothers and sisters, at this point we must acknowledge that we are not always faithful witnesses.

Sometimes we are fearful. Sometimes we are greedy. Sometimes we know the truth clearly and yet refuse to obey.

Who, then, can save us?

Thanks be to God! Revelation has already directed our eyes to the One who is truly and perfectly faithful.

Revelation 1:5 calls Jesus Christ:

> “the faithful witness” (Revelation 1:5, KJV, excerpt).

He came into the world proclaiming the gospel of the kingdom. He called sinners to repentance, exposed religious hypocrisy, and showed mercy to sinners whom others despised.

He did not change the truth to win the world's welcome. He did not abandon His Father's will to escape suffering.

In the end, He was rejected and nailed to a cross.

Brothers and sisters, consider this: the holy, sinless Savior willingly bore the shame of the cross to save sinners.

He was not punished for sins of His own. He laid down His life for our sins.

We often compromise to preserve our respectability. He willingly endured shame for us. We often evade responsibility to protect our interests. He willingly went to the cross to save us.

But, brothers and sisters, the cross is not the end of the story.

On the third day, He rose from the dead!

Sin could not overcome Him. Death could not hold Him. Darkness could not extinguish His light.

This is the foundation of the church's witness.

Our courage does not earn God's acceptance. Because Christ has already accomplished salvation for us, we have the courage to repent and stand again.

We do not manufacture spiritual power through our own zeal. Through the Holy Spirit given by the risen Christ, we are enabled to continue as His witnesses.

The Lord Jesus says:

> “But ye shall receive power, after that the Holy Ghost is come upon you: and ye shall be witnesses unto me” (Acts 1:8, KJV, excerpt).

Brothers and sisters, the church's true power enables the world to see Jesus Christ through our lives, rather than simply admire our success.

## Conclusion: Are You Willing to Take Off the World's Outer Garment?

Brothers and sisters, today's vision of the two witnesses places three questions before us.

First, clothed in sackcloth: when God calls you to repent, are you willing to turn away from sin?

Do not use religious enthusiasm to conceal an unwillingness to repent.

Second, standing before the Lord: when the world's resources and securities can no longer be relied upon, will you still depend on God's Spirit?

Do not make money, status, and technology the foundation of spiritual life.

Third, fire from their mouths: when the world rejects the truth, are you still willing to bear faithful witness to Christ?

Do not hide God's Word to gain people's approval.

Brothers and sisters, the Lord is not calling us to seek persecution deliberately or to attack the world harshly.

He calls us to repent sincerely in an age of compromise, depend humbly on the Holy Spirit in an age that worships power, and bear witness to Christ courageously and lovingly in an age that rejects truth.

Perhaps you do not need to put on literal sackcloth. But you need a heart willing to repent.

Perhaps you will not experience the special miracles of the two witnesses. But you need the strength the Holy Spirit gives.

Perhaps you will not face the danger of martyrdom. But every day you face choices about faithfulness to Christ.

Brothers and sisters, pursue more than being a respectable Christian. Seek to be a true Christian.

Come to the cross today. Lay down the false mask. Acknowledge your weakness. Leave unrighteous ways. Trust anew in the Lord who laid down His life for us and rose from the dead.

Let us become people who truly bear witness to Christ in our homes, workplaces, society, and the world, extending our faith beyond the sanctuary.

Darkness may surround the lampstand, but it cannot change the lampstand's calling to shine.

May we bear faithful witness until Christ reigns!

Amen.

“God has not called the church to become the most powerful organization in the world. He has called the church to be a lampstand bearing faithful witness to Christ in the darkness.”

“Brothers and sisters, the church's greatest danger today may be that, before the world has even told us to be quiet, we have already chosen silence for the sake of profit, respectability, and comfort.”

## Closing Prayer

Holy, righteous, and loving heavenly Father:

We come before You, confessing that we often seek the world's approval more than Your pleasure, and look after our own interests more than we obey Your truth.

Forgive our hidden sins and give us hearts of true repentance.

Help us to rely on the Holy Spirit, rather than on money, status, or human ability, and faithfully fulfill the mission You have entrusted to us.

When we are weak, turn our eyes to the cross. When we are fearful, remind us that the risen Lord has already won the victory.

May our church be more than activities and organization. Make us a church faithful to Scripture, loving one another, and courageously bearing witness to Christ.

We pray in the holy name of the Lord Jesus Christ. Amen.
