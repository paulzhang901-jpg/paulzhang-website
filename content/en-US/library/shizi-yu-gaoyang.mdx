# The Lion and the Lamb

## Victory Comes Not Through Force, but Through the Faithfulness of the Cross

**Scripture: Revelation 5:5--7 (KJV; focus on 5:5--6)**

> **Revelation 5:5** --- And one of the elders saith unto me, Weep not:
> behold, the Lion of the tribe of Juda, the Root of David, hath
> prevailed to open the book, and to loose the seven seals thereof.\
> **Revelation 5:6** --- And I beheld, and, lo, in the midst of the
> throne and of the four beasts, and in the midst of the elders, stood a
> Lamb as it had been slain, having seven horns and seven eyes, which
> are the seven Spirits of God sent forth into all the earth.\
> **Revelation 5:7** --- And he came and took the book out of the right
> hand of him that sat upon the throne.

**Big Idea: The true Victor is the Lion of the tribe of Judah, yet He is
revealed as the Lamb who was slain, who lives, and who reigns.
Therefore, in an age of pressure, the church does not overcome through
fleshly control, but through truth, self-giving love, and
faithfulness.**

## Introduction: Why Do We Always Want to Be the "Lion"?

Brothers and sisters, we live in a world that places tremendous emphasis
on winning.

At home, we want our spouse to listen to us and our children to follow
our plans. At work, we want people to respect us and not take credit for
what we have done. In relationships, we want to prove that we are right.
Even in the church, we may want our opinions to be adopted and our
service to be recognized.

When things do not go our way, it is easy to become forceful. We raise
our voice. Our attitude grows cold. We increase the pressure. We tighten
our control. Sometimes we use silence, sarcasm, the cold shoulder, or
even spiritual language to force others to give in.

We tell ourselves, "I am defending principle. I am defending truth. I am
protecting the church." But often what we are actually defending is not
truth. It is our reputation, our authority, and our sense of control.

Why do we want so badly to win?

Because we are afraid. We worry that if we do not control things,
everything will fall apart; if we do not protect ourselves, someone will
hurt us; if we do not fight for ourselves, no one will speak for us.

So we want to become the lion ourselves.

But Revelation 5 gives us a stunning spiritual reversal.

John hears about a Lion, but when he turns, he sees a Lamb. He hears of
victory, but sees One who has been slain. He hears of a King, but sees a
sacrificial Lamb.

This is not a contradiction. It is the mystery of the gospel:

**The true Lion conquers in the way of the Lamb.**

## I. John Hears of the Lion: God's Promised King Has Never Lost Control

"Behold, the Lion of the tribe of Juda, the Root of David, hath
prevailed." (Rev. 5:5)

At the beginning of Revelation 5, John sees a scroll in the right hand
of the One seated on the throne, sealed with seven seals. The scroll is
closely bound up with God's purpose for history, judgment, redemption,
and the fulfillment of His kingdom. Yet no one is worthy to open it.

A mighty angel cries out, "Who is worthy to open the book, and to loose
the seals thereof?" (Rev. 5:2, KJV).

No one in heaven, on earth, or under the earth is able to open it or
even look upon it. John begins to weep deeply.

Why?

If no one can open the scroll, history appears unable to move toward the
end God has promised. Evil seems as though it may never be judged. Those
who suffer seem as though they may never receive justice. Imperial power
appears able to rule indefinitely, while the oppressed church appears
destined only to lose.

### 1. The Real Pressure Facing the First-Century Churches

Revelation was written to churches in first-century Asia Minor. They
lived under the political, economic, social, and religious pressures of
the Roman imperial world. Emperor worship and related social practices
could place Christians who remained loyal to Christ under pressure,
exposing them to exclusion, economic loss, slander, and at times
persecution.

From a human perspective, Rome looked like the lion. It possessed
armies, law, wealth, and coercive power. The church looked small,
marginal, and politically powerless.

While John weeps because history seems to have lost its way, one of the
elders says:

"Weep not: behold, the Lion of the tribe of Juda, the Root of David,
hath prevailed."

"Weep not" is not cheap comfort. It does not mean, "Try to think
positively."

It is heaven's announcement:

**Do not weep, because the true King has already conquered.**

### 2. "The Lion of the Tribe of Judah"

This title recalls Jacob's blessing over Judah in Genesis 49:9--10,
where lion imagery and the ruler's scepter express the hope of royal
rule through Judah. That royal hope is later connected with God's
covenant promise concerning David's house (2 Sam. 7:12--16).

"The Root of David" also echoes Isaiah's hope for the messianic King
from David's line (see Isa. 11:1, 10).

Revelation 5:5 therefore announces that God's promise of the Messiah and
His kingdom has not failed. Rome is not the final master of history.
Caesar is not the final king. Jesus Christ is the everlasting King.

### 3. Why Do We Keep Trying to Become the Lion?

Brothers and sisters, we must not simply equate the experience of modern
Christians with the situation of the first-century churches.

A slight injustice at the office is not necessarily Roman-style
persecution. Someone disagreeing with us does not mean we are suffering
for the truth. A spouse refusing to follow our wishes does not make us a
persecuted martyr.

Often our forcefulness is not a defense of truth. It is the old self
wanting to be king.

When our children do not follow our plans, we immediately become
anxious. When coworkers or fellow servants do not accept our ideas, we
become angry. When people misunderstand us, we rush to defend ourselves.
When the future becomes uncertain, we try to grab hold of everything.

It is almost as though we were saying:

"Lord, if I do not take over, perhaps even You will not be able to
control this situation."

That is why we keep trying to become the lion.

### Application: Return to the Sovereignty of Christ

Revelation does not first teach us how to win. It first shows us who has
already won.

You do not need to be the savior of your family. You do not need to be
the savior of the church. You do not need to carry the weight of
history.

You must be faithful, but you do not have to control everything.

When things do not go your way, do not immediately raise your voice or
try to control other people. Ask first:

**"Am I responding this way because I honor Christ as King, or because I
want to be king myself?"**

Hearing about the Lion means that we do not need to play the lion
ourselves. Christ already reigns.

## II. John Sees the Lamb: The Kingdom Conquers Through the Self-Giving Way of the Cross

"Stood a Lamb as it had been slain." (Rev. 5:6)

Brothers and sisters, imagine John's astonishment.

The elder has told him, "Do not weep! The Lion of the tribe of Judah has
conquered!"

Yet when John turns toward the throne, what he sees is a Lamb---and not
an untouched Lamb, but a Lamb "as it had been slain."

This overturns a world that worships coercive power.

The world says, "The strong are the ones who rule." The cross reveals
that the self-giving Lamb is the true King.\
The world says, "Victory means destroying your enemies." The gospel
reveals Christ conquering sin and death through the cross.\
The world understands power as making others bleed. The Lamb sheds His
own blood to redeem others.

### 1. Slain, Yet Standing

The Greek word behind "slain" comes from **σφάζω (sphazō)**, a strong
term for slaughter. The image describes a death that is real and
violent; within the context of Revelation 5, that death is also
redemptive.

Christ was truly rejected, truly humiliated, truly crucified, truly shed
His blood, and truly died.

Yet the text also says that the Lamb is standing.

John sees a Lamb bearing the marks of slaughter, yet alive and standing
in the midst of the throne.

**Slain points us to the cross; standing proclaims that He lives and has
conquered.**

The wounds remain, but death has lost.

Christ is not a powerless victim. He is the Victor who has passed
through death and overcome it.

### 2. How Does the Lion Conquer? Through the Self-Giving Victory of the Lamb

The Lion and the Lamb are not two Christs, nor are they two conflicting
personalities within Christ.

John hears of the Lion and then sees the slain-yet-standing Lamb. This
literary reversal binds Christ's royal victory to His self-giving death.

He did not establish the kingdom of God by imitating Roman imperial
power. Through the cross He defeated sin and death. He did not establish
His kingdom through hatred, but purchased a people with His own blood.

Revelation 5:9 soon explains why the Lamb is worthy:

"Thou wast slain, and hast redeemed us to God by thy blood out of every
kindred, and tongue, and people, and nation."

His road to victory passes through being slain and shedding His blood.
The fruit of His victory is the redemption of sinners and a people
gathered to God from the nations.

### 3. The Way of the Lamb Is Not a "Victim Mentality"

We must be clear here. Lamb-like victory does not mean labeling every
unpleasant experience as persecution. Nor does it mean encouraging
people to remain passively in violence, abuse, or danger without seeking
help.

Following the Lamb does not excuse evil, and it does not abandon
justice. Christians may seek protection, assistance, and appropriate
means of justice. The issue is that we refuse to allow fleshly anger,
hatred, and revenge to become the powers that shape us.

The way of the cross is not weakness for weakness's sake. It is faithful
truth and love under the sovereignty of God, refusing to reproduce the
violent logic of the world.

## III. Seven Horns and Seven Eyes: The Slain Lamb Still Reigns Completely

Revelation 5:6 portrays the Lamb as having "seven horns and seven eyes,
which are the seven Spirits of God sent forth into all the earth."

In biblical imagery, a horn is often associated with strength and
authority, while seven frequently communicates completeness in
Revelation. The seven horns therefore portray the Lamb's complete power.

The seven eyes are connected with "the seven Spirits of God." The Lamb
is not blind to the suffering of the earth. His rule is not ignorant,
distant, or out of control.

The seven Spirits of God are sent forth into all the earth, showing that
the Lamb's reign is active. The Spirit sustains the church, searches
hearts, empowers witness, and advances the Lamb's kingdom among the
nations.

The way of the Lamb, therefore, is not passive resignation to defeat. It
is faithful witness in the power of the Holy Spirit.

### Three Marks of Lamb-Like Victory

#### First, Do Not Compromise the Truth

The Lamb is gentle, but He never compromises with sin.

The church cannot change the gospel simply to gain the world's
acceptance. We cannot blur biblical truth for the sake of numbers,
resources, or relationships.

But standing for truth does not require a harsh spirit. Paul tells us to
speak "the truth in love" (Eph. 4:15).

**Love without truth becomes indulgence; truth without love becomes a
weapon.**

#### Second, Do Not Retaliate in Hatred

Jesus did not allow retaliation to define His path when He faced
persecution.

This does not mean evil has no consequences, nor does it mean Christians
cannot seek justice. It means we refuse to let hatred decide what kind
of people we become.

Romans 12:21 says:

"Be not overcome of evil, but overcome evil with good."

If someone attacks you with slander and you answer with slander, you may
win an argument for the moment, but you may already have allowed that
person's evil to reshape you.

Lamb-like victory refuses to let hatred determine our character.

#### Third, Do Not Abandon Faithfulness When Suffering Comes

The Lamb was slain, yet He stands.

Faithfulness sometimes has a cost. Honesty may cost you an opportunity.
Forgiveness may not immediately produce an apology. A church that holds
to the gospel may, for a time, be misunderstood by society.

But Revelation calls the church not to withdraw because of suffering and
not to compromise because of pressure.

Not because we are naturally strong, but because the Lamb with seven
horns and seven eyes reigns.

### Application: This Is Not Losing; It Is a Declaration of Faith

When you restrain your tongue and refuse to slander, you are not weak.
You are declaring: the Lamb sees.

When you refuse dishonesty at work even though it may cost you, you are
not foolish. You are declaring: the Lamb's authority is greater than any
human position.

When you end the cold war in a family conflict and begin to speak
honestly and gently, you are not surrendering. You are declaring: the
cross is more powerful than control.

When you have been hurt, seek justice, and yet refuse to be consumed by
hatred, you are not excusing evil. You are declaring: final judgment
belongs to the Lamb.

Because the Lamb with seven horns and seven eyes reigns, we can be
gentle without being cowardly, patient without compromising, and willing
to suffer without abandoning faithfulness.

## Conclusion: History's Final Chapter Is Not Written by Coercive Power, but Unfolded by the Lamb

Brothers and sisters, Revelation 5 gives us a picture we must not
forget.

John hears of the Lion and sees the Lamb. The Lamb appears slain, yet He
stands. He has seven horns, portraying complete power. He has seven
eyes, and the seven Spirits of God are sent forth into all the earth. He
comes and takes the scroll from the One seated on the throne.

History's final chapter, therefore, is not written by Caesar. It is not
written by empire. It is not written by money, political power, or
violence. Under the sovereignty of God, history is unfolded by the Lamb
who was slain and yet stands.

Therefore, we do not have to play the lion ourselves.

We do not have to prove ourselves by being louder. We do not have to
control others in order to feel secure. We do not have to retaliate in
order to recover our dignity. We do not have to interpret every
disagreement as persecution.

We need only follow the Lamb faithfully:

**Do not compromise the truth. Do not retaliate in hatred. Do not
abandon faithfulness in suffering.**

True victory is not pushing other people down, but seeing the old self
crucified. It is not winning every argument, but allowing Christ to be
seen in every circumstance. It is not controlling the outcome by force,
but entrusting ourselves to the Lamb with seven horns and seven eyes.

## Call: Are You Playing the Lion, or Following the Lamb?

Take a quiet moment today and ask:

In my family, am I using anger, silence, or control to force others to
submit?\
At work, am I disguising retaliation as justice?\
In the church, am I disguising my personal opinion as spiritual truth?\
When I face uncertainty, am I grasping for control because I do not
really trust that God reigns?

The Lord is not calling you to become weak. He is inviting you to lay
down fleshly power and receive the true strength of the Lamb.

Bring one specific situation before Him and pray:

> Lord, I will no longer try to play the lion. Teach me to follow the
> Lamb: uncompromising in truth, refusing revenge in love, and faithful
> in suffering. I believe You have seven horns and seven eyes. You see,
> You reign, and You will judge with righteousness.

## Closing Prayer

Lord Jesus, You are the Lion of the tribe of Judah and the Lamb who was
slain and lives again.

We confess that many times fear makes us want to control, pride makes us
want to win, and unbelief makes us want to control the outcome
ourselves. We even disguise fleshly anger as truth, retaliation as
justice, and forcefulness as spiritual courage.

Forgive us.

Lord, help us truly to see You. You appear as slain, yet You stand. You
have seven horns, possessing complete authority. You have seven eyes,
and the seven Spirits of God are sent forth into all the earth.

Make us faithful to truth without compromise, loving without revenge,
and steadfast without abandoning our calling when suffering comes. Help
us lay down control in our families, remain honest at work, serve humbly
in the church, and entrust ourselves to the One who judges righteously
when we are misunderstood.

We believe that history's final outcome is not in the hands of coercive
powers, but in the hands of the Lamb. May we bear faithful witness
through the blood of the Lamb until the day we see You face to face.

In the name of Jesus Christ our Lord we pray. Amen.
