- **Scripture:** Revelation 7:1–8
- **Subtitle:** The world may shake, but those who belong to the Lamb will not be forgotten.
- **Key verse:** “And I saw another angel ascending from the east, having the seal of the living God.” (Revelation 7:2, KJV)
- **Central message:** Heaven and earth may shake, and suffering may come, but God knows those who belong to him. His seal is not a charm that shields us from suffering; it declares our identity, holiness, and final preservation.
- **Key sentence:** The world may not know who you are, but God’s seal declares: you belong to the Lamb.

## Introduction: Chapter 6 Asks Who Can Stand; Chapter 7 Begins to Answer

Brothers and sisters, we cannot read Revelation chapter 7 apart from chapter 6.

At the end of chapter 6, heaven and earth shake. Kings, great men, military commanders, the rich, the powerful, and everyone enslaved or free hide themselves. The chapter closes with a staggering question:

> “For the great day of his wrath is come; and who shall be able to stand?” (Revelation 6:17, KJV)

Chapter 7 unfolds in the wake of that question.

**Who can stand?**

Not those with the greatest ability.  
Not those with the greatest wealth.  
Not those with the strongest political connections.  
And not those who are best at protecting themselves.

Chapter 7 first directs our attention to a people sealed by God.

This does not mean that God’s people will never go through storms. It tells us that **before the storm arrives, God already knows who belongs to him.**

In other words:

Chapter 6 shows us **how the world is shaken**.  
Chapter 7 shows us **how God treats those who belong to him**.

Today we will consider three things in this passage:

**First, judgment is held back—God still rules the world and sustains history by his grace.**  
**Second, the seal on the forehead—God knows those who belong to him and calls them to be holy.**  
**Third, the 144,000—God counts his people, and not one will be forgotten.**

## I. Judgment Is Held Back—God Still Rules the World and Sustains History by His Grace

**Revelation 7:1–3**

Verse 1 says:

> “And after these things I saw four angels standing on the four corners of the earth, holding the four winds of the earth, that the wind should not blow on the earth, nor on the sea, nor on any tree.” (KJV)

Here is a very important picture:

**The winds are being held back.**

The winds are ready.  
The winds have the power to blow.  
But they cannot blow whenever they please.

Why?

Because they must wait within the timing and limits God has set.

In verses 2–3, an angel ascending from the east, carrying the seal of the living God, cries out:

> “Hurt not the earth, neither the sea, nor the trees, till we have sealed the servants of our God in their foreheads.” (KJV)

Notice two expressions:

**“Hurt not.”**  
**“Till.”**

Judgment is not an uncontrolled force erupting at will. Nor do disasters themselves determine the course of history.

**God still governs the timing, the reach, and the limits.**

### 1. This is true of chapter 6’s four horsemen, and of chapter 7’s four winds

In chapter 6, the horsemen do not possess authority independently. They act under permission.

In chapter 7, the four winds are likewise restrained.

So Revelation keeps telling churches under pressure and in distress:

> **Disaster has power, but not ultimate sovereignty. The world may shake, but the throne does not.**

We easily make this mistake:

**We see the wind but fail to see the hand holding it back.**

When we see war, economic upheaval, disease, and a society tearing apart, it is easy to ask:

“Has this world spun out of control?”

Revelation’s answer is:

**No.**

God has not explained why every disaster happens. But he repeatedly shows us that history ultimately remains under his sovereignty.

### 2. “Hurt not the earth, neither the sea, nor the trees”—God’s grace still sustains creation

There is another important detail here.

The angel says:

> “Hurt not the earth, neither the sea, nor the trees.” (KJV)

Earth, sea, and trees bring into view the whole created world and the order on which human life depends.

We should not take for granted that the world continues to function today.

The sun still rises.  
The rain still falls.  
The land still produces food.  
People can still work, eat, and establish families.  
The church can still proclaim the gospel.

Genesis 8:22 records God’s promise concerning the created order after the flood:

> “While the earth remaineth, seedtime and harvest, and cold and heat, and summer and winter, and day and night shall not cease.” (KJV)

The Lord Jesus also says that the Father:

> “Maketh his sun to rise on the evil and on the good, and sendeth rain on the just and on the unjust.” (Matthew 5:45, KJV)

Throughout Scripture, we see God’s continuing providence and grace toward creation.

Revelation chapter 7 specifically emphasizes that judgment is temporarily restrained until God’s servants have been sealed.

At the very least, then, this passage reminds us:

**God still sustains history, and his redemptive purpose is still moving forward.**

### 3. Today is still a time of grace and a time for mission

Second Peter 3:9 says:

> “The Lord is not slack concerning his promise, as some men count slackness; but is longsuffering to us-ward … but that all should come to repentance.” (KJV)

So when we consider God’s patience in history in light of the whole New Testament, we cannot understand the time we still have merely as time to earn money, buy a house, or enjoy life.

For the church, it is also **time to repent, time to bear witness, and time to share the gospel.**

So let us not simply ask, “When will this world become even more chaotic?”

We ought to ask instead:

> **“God is still giving me time today. How am I using it?”**

Am I still praying for family members who do not believe?  
Am I making use of opportunities to share the gospel?  
Am I simply enjoying God’s grace without participating in his mission?

> **God’s restraining of the four winds shows us not only that he still reigns. It also reminds us that today is still a time of grace and mission.**

## II. The Seal on the Forehead—God Knows Those Who Belong to Him and Calls Them to Be Holy

**Revelation 7:2–3**

Verse 2 says, “Having the seal of the living God.”  
Verse 3 says, “Till we have sealed the servants of our God in their foreheads.” (KJV)

The Greek word for “seal” here is **σφραγίς (sphragis)**.

In the ancient world, a seal could convey ownership, authenticity, authority, and belonging.

One of the most important messages here, therefore, is:

> **“You belong to God.”**

### 1. The seal first declares identity and belonging

The world is constantly attaching labels to us. A company says, “You are an employee.” A bank measures your credit with a number. Schools measure your educational background with credentials. Society often evaluates a person in terms of success or failure.

But God’s seal places these secondary identities beneath a greater identity:

> **You belong to God.**

That is a Christian’s ultimate identity.

Ephesians 1:13 says:

> “In whom ye also trusted, after that ye heard the word of truth, the gospel of your salvation: in whom also after that ye believed, ye were sealed with that holy Spirit of promise.” (KJV)

When we understand the seal within the New Testament’s broader context of salvation, a believer’s identity as one who belongs to God is closely connected to the work of the Holy Spirit.

### 2. The seal is not insurance against suffering

God’s seal does not mean, “From now on, you will never experience any suffering.”

The saints in later chapters of Revelation still face persecution, pressure, tribulation, and even death.

Jesus says:

> “In the world ye shall have tribulation: but be of good cheer; I have overcome the world.” (John 16:33, KJV)

So the assurance given by the seal should not be reduced to “Your circumstances cannot hurt you.”

It declares something deeper:

> **“Whatever your circumstances, you still belong to God.”**

Suffering can touch our bodies. The world can take away some of what we possess. But none of these things can redefine whom we ultimately belong to.

Jesus says:

> “Neither shall any man pluck them out of my hand.” (John 10:28, KJV)

Therefore:

> **God’s seal is not a charm for escaping suffering. It is the mark that we still belong to him in the midst of suffering.**

### 3. Ezekiel’s background reminds us: those who belong to God must not become numb to sin

Ezekiel 9:4 records:

> “Go through the midst of the city, through the midst of Jerusalem, and set a mark upon the foreheads of the men that sigh and that cry for all the abominations that be done in the midst thereof.” (KJV)

This is not exactly the same scene as Revelation chapter 7. But the mark on the forehead provides an important Old Testament background.

Belonging to God should do more than make us feel secure. It should change our response to sin.

When the world dresses up greed as success, something within you is unsettled.  
When the world dresses up deception as cleverness, you know that is not the Lamb’s way.  
When the world dresses up sexual immorality as freedom, you cannot stop discerning by God’s word simply because the culture accepts it.  
When the world encourages hatred, revenge, and fleshly anger, the Holy Spirit reminds you:

> **“You belong to the Lamb.”**

### 4. The seal on the forehead reinforces Revelation’s theme of ultimate belonging

Later in Revelation, another vivid contrast appears: the mark of the beast.

Again and again, the book puts this question before its readers:

**Whom do you really belong to?**

The seal in Revelation should therefore stir more than curiosity about end-times symbols. It should confront us with how we live today:

> **If I belong to the Lamb, how should I live today?**

When your workplace asks you to sacrifice your integrity—remember that you belong to the Lamb.  
When conflict tempts you to retaliate—remember that you belong to the Lamb.  
When money tempts you to cross a line—remember that you belong to the Lamb.  
When society tells you, “Everybody does it,” a Christian must still ask, “But what pleases my Lord?”

> **God’s seal is not there to help us escape suffering. It enables us to know, even in suffering, whom we belong to—and therefore to live in holiness and faithfulness.**

## III. The 144,000—God Counts His People, and Not One Will Be Forgotten

**Revelation 7:4–8**

Verse 4 says:

> “And I heard the number of them which were sealed: and there were sealed an hundred and forty and four thousand of all the tribes of the children of Israel.” (KJV)

This passage has long received different interpretations. We need both exegetical conviction and exegetical humility.

### 1. There are at least two major interpretations of the 144,000

One interpretation understands this primarily as a group of Israelites specially sealed by God in the last days. The reasoning is straightforward: the passage explicitly says “all the tribes of the children of Israel” and then lists twelve tribes. Those who hold this interpretation often connect the passage with the discussion of Israel in Romans chapter 11.

Another major interpretation understands 144,000 as a symbolic number representing the complete redeemed people of God. **12 × 12 × 1,000 = 144,000.** The number twelve brings to mind Israel’s twelve tribes and the twelve apostles. The New Jerusalem in Revelation 21:12–14 likewise includes both the twelve tribes and the twelve apostles.

There is a genuine interpretive disagreement between these views. We should not pretend it does not exist.

Yet whichever of these major interpretations we adopt, the passage has at least one unmistakable shared emphasis:

> **God knows who belongs to him.**

### 2. The list of twelve tribes is not an ordinary census table

This list of tribes is unusual.

**Judah comes first.**  
**Dan is absent.**  
**Levi is included.**  
**Joseph and Manasseh both appear.**  
**Ephraim is not named directly.**

This is not simply a copy of some standard Old Testament tribal list.

Why does Judah appear first? It naturally recalls Revelation 5:5: “The Lion of the tribe of Juda … hath prevailed.” (KJV)

The list therefore has, at the very least, a clear Christological background: **God’s people are ultimately known and defined within the Lamb’s redemption.**

There have been different explanations for why Dan and Ephraim are not listed under their own names. **Revelation 7:4–8 alone does not warrant dogmatically assigning one exclusive reason.**

### 3. “I heard the number”—an important literary anticipation

Verse 4 contains an expression worth noticing:

> **“And I heard …”** (KJV)

Then verse 9 says:

> **“After this I beheld …”** (KJV)

He sees “a great multitude, which no man could number, of all nations, and kindreds, and people, and tongues …” (KJV)

This creates a striking literary transition:

**Heard—the 144,000.**  
**Seen—a countless multitude from every nation.**

It reminds us of Revelation chapter 5. John hears of “the Lion of the tribe of Juda,” but when he looks, he sees “a Lamb as it had been slain.” (KJV)

Some interpreters therefore understand the 144,000 in 7:4 and the countless multitude in 7:9 as two perspectives on God’s redeemed people. Others understand them as distinct groups.

**The passage itself does not require every reader to adopt one of these interpretations.**

Even so, this literary structure gives us a powerful picture:

> **John hears of a precisely counted people and then sees a redeemed multitude beyond anyone’s counting.**

What he hears is a count; what he sees is a harvest.  
What he hears is completeness; what he sees is vastness.

### 4. God counts his people because the Shepherd knows his sheep

The comfort of the 144,000 is not merely whether we can decode the number.

What matters more is this:

**God can count them.**

We cannot count them all, but God can. We see a crowd; God knows his own sheep.

Jesus says:

> “I am the good shepherd, and know my sheep, and am known of mine.” (John 10:14, KJV)

The first-century church may have looked small, weak, and marginal. The empire may not have known their names, and history may have overlooked them.

But God says:

> **“I know you.”**

You are not a number on God’s spreadsheet. You are someone he knows, someone he has called, someone who belongs to him.

> **The people we cannot count, God knows one by one. History may omit their names, but the Lamb will not forget those who belong to him.**

## Conclusion: From “Who Can Stand?” to “Whom Do You Belong To?”

Revelation 7:1–8 shows us at least three things.

**First, the winds will blow, but God still reigns.**

**Second, suffering will come, but it cannot redefine the identity of those who belong to God.**

**Third, the world may forget us, but God knows those who belong to him.**

Chapter 6 asks, “Who shall be able to stand?” (KJV)

Chapter 7 begins by directing our attention to those who belong to God and have been sealed by him.

But the story is not over.

In verse 4, John says, “And I heard …”  
In verse 9, he will say, “After this I beheld …” (KJV)

He will see an uncountable multitude from every nation, tribe, people, and language, standing before the throne and before the Lamb.

So if you feel small today, do not lose heart.  
If you feel that no one sees your faithfulness, do not lose heart.  
If you feel lonely in a world where faithfulness to Christ grows harder, do not lose heart either.

History is not ultimately moving toward the disappearance of the Lamb’s people.

The final scene Revelation shows us is the great worship gathering of all nations and peoples before God and the Lamb.

**You stand not because you are strong, but because you belong to the Lamb.**

> **The world may not know who you are, but God’s seal declares: you belong to the Lamb.**

Next time, we will move from “I heard the number” and join John in seeing the multitude no one can count.

## A Prayer of Response

Dear heavenly Father,

Thank you that you still govern history. Thank you that, before judgment comes, you still give this world a time of grace, an opportunity to repent, and the mission of proclaiming the gospel.

Forgive us for so often taking everything we have today for granted, and for so often spending the time you give us only on ourselves.

Lord, thank you for redeeming us through Christ and for sealing those who belong to you through the Holy Spirit.

Do not let us treat your protection as cheap insurance. Help us truly live as people who belong to the Lamb.

When this world calls sin normal, keep our hearts in reverence for your word. When this world demands compromise, help us remember whom we belong to. When money, success, power, and pleasure try to redefine us, enable us to say firmly:

**“I belong to the Lamb.”**

Lord, when we feel lonely, small, and unseen, remind us that you know every person who belongs to you.

Keep us faithful to the end, until the day we stand before your throne with that countless multitude from every nation, tribe, people, and language, proclaiming with a loud voice:

> “Salvation to our God which sitteth upon the throne, and unto the Lamb.” (Revelation 7:10, KJV)

We pray in the name of the Lord Jesus Christ.

**Amen.**
