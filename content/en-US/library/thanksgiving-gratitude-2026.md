# Gratitude on Thanksgiving

*There Is a Special Kind of Happiness in Becoming One Family in Christ*

**By Paul Zhang (张崇助)**

November 26, 2026 — Thanksgiving Day in the United States.

Every Thanksgiving, Americans make every effort to return home. No matter how demanding their work may be or how far their children have moved away, families long to gather around the same table, enjoy a roast turkey, share the stories of the past year, and count the blessings they have received.

Thanksgiving is about more than an abundant meal. It invites busy people to stop for a moment and rediscover the quiet gifts of ordinary life—gifts that are so easily overlooked.

For those of us who are first-generation Chinese immigrants living in the United States, however, Thanksgiving carries a special meaning.

## 1. Far from Our Homeland, We Have Become a Family

Many first-generation Chinese immigrants live far from their hometowns and the relatives and friends they once knew so well. Some still have parents in China. Others have children scattered across different cities. Still others face life's joys and sorrows alone in a country that once felt unfamiliar.

That is why our church has developed a cherished Thanksgiving tradition: brothers and sisters gather at church to celebrate the holiday together.

In one sense, our celebration differs from the familiar American tradition. While many people return to their immediate families, we come together as the larger family of the church.

We come from different places and carry different stories. Our Mandarin bears the accents of different hometowns, and our daily habits are not always the same. Yet a shared faith has brought us together and made us brothers and sisters in one another's lives.

The most beautiful human relationships do not always begin with blood ties.

Sometimes people who were once complete strangers become a family in the truest sense through a common faith, a shared hope, and a willingness to care for one another.

In a foreign land, to know that someone remembers you, walks beside you, prays for you, and supports you when you are weak is itself a gift worthy of thanksgiving.

Our homeland is where we were born. Home is where our lives find a place to belong.

## 2. This Year's Most Beautiful Gift: Seven Baptisms

This Thanksgiving was especially meaningful for our church.

Today, we celebrated the baptisms of seven people: five adult brothers and sisters and two lovely children.

As a pastor, I experience a joy that is difficult to put into words whenever I baptize someone.

Baptism is more than a church ceremony. It is a public declaration of belonging, an important moment in which a person responds to God's grace and steps forward on the journey of faith.

Life offers many days worth remembering: a birthday, a graduation, a wedding, or a professional achievement. For a Christian, the day of baptism is also a day to remember for a lifetime.

From that day forward, a believer is reminded not only where life began, but also to whom that life belongs and in what direction it should be lived.

As I watched these seven people standing at the front of the church in their white baptismal garments, holding flowers, my heart overflowed with gratitude.

For a church, the greatest cause for joy is not simply an increase in attendance. It is seeing one life after another begin to know Christ, follow Christ, and grow steadily in the truth.

## 3. A Grandmother's Deepest Wish

Among the seven people baptized today were two children whose story moved me especially deeply.

Their grandmother once asked me, “Pastor, can children be baptized? I very much hope that my two grandsons can be baptized into the name of the Lord.”

She told me that the boys had grown up close to her. Along with caring for their daily needs, she often prayed with them and planted seeds of faith in their young hearts.

Sometimes, rather amusingly, the grandsons were the ones reminding her:

“Grandma, I'm going to bed, and you haven't prayed for me yet!”

What a simple sentence—and how deeply it touched me.

Prayer was no longer merely something their grandmother told them to do. Little by little, it had become part of their own lives. In ways appropriate to their age, they had also begun to understand that Jesus is their Savior.

This grandmother carried another concern. In the future, her son might move with his family to another place because of work. If that happened, she might no longer have as many opportunities to be with her grandsons.

While she still had time to accompany them, she wanted to plant the seeds of faith deeply in their hearts.

Of course, like any loving grandmother, she hopes that her grandsons will be healthy, intelligent, successful in school, and blessed with good lives. But beyond all these hopes, she has a deeper longing: that they will know and follow the Lord throughout their lives and never forget the truth they received as children, wherever they may go.

Her desire reminded me of the words of Proverbs:

> “Train up a child in the way he should go; even when he is old he will not depart from it.”
>
> — Proverbs 22:6 (ESV)

## 4. What Are We Really Leaving to the Next Generation?

Christian traditions differ in their understanding and practice of baptizing children. Scripture does not prescribe a specific minimum age for baptism. Yet Jesus' attitude toward children is unmistakably clear:

> “Let the children come to me; do not hinder them, for to such belongs the kingdom of God.”
>
> — Mark 10:14 (ESV)

After speaking with this grandmother, and considering the children's existing understanding of faith and the years she had spent nurturing them spiritually, I encouraged her to take on the responsibility of a godmother—to continue walking with the boys as they grow in the truth.

Baptism is not the end of Christian formation. It is an important beginning on the lifelong path of discipleship.

Today's baptism calls for tomorrow's companionship, teaching, prayer, and example.

Her wish also prompted me to consider a larger question: **What, ultimately, should our generation leave to the next?**

Chinese parents and grandparents often give everything they have to save money for their children, buy property, secure the best possible education, and create opportunities for the next generation. Some even sacrifice their own comfort in the hope of making their children's future easier.

These efforts certainly have value.

Yet we may leave children wealth without being able to ensure that they will use it wisely. We may provide an education without being able to guarantee wisdom. We may give them excellent living conditions without being able to promise them a happy life.

In the end, we cannot accompany our children every step of the way, nor can we face every storm on their behalf.

But we can plant the seeds of truth in their lives while they are young. Then, when they encounter temptation, disappointment, failure, or suffering, they may know whom to trust and what to hold fast.

**To leave children material wealth is to help prepare the conditions of their lives. To pass on faith is to help lay the foundation on which they can face a lifetime.**

Perhaps this is the most precious spiritual inheritance a grandmother can leave her grandsons.

## 5. A Table Full of Food, a Room Full of Warmth

Of course, no Thanksgiving celebration would be complete without delicious food.

Brothers and sisters brought dishes they had prepared. Fruit, cakes, snacks, and all kinds of wonderful food filled the long tables.

Each person brought something different. But when everyone's generosity came together, it became a feast.

This, too, is a beautiful picture of church life.

Some prepared food. Others arranged the room, served, or contributed quietly behind the scenes. Each person's offering might have seemed small, but together those offerings became a blessing to everyone.

True abundance is measured not only by how much one person possesses, but also by how much a community is willing to share.

Today, we enjoyed more than physical nourishment. We experienced the joy of fellowship.

The food on the table satisfied our appetites. The care we showed one another warmed our hearts. And God's grace gave our lives a deeper satisfaction still.

## 6. Gratitude Does Not Mean Everything Has Gone Our Way

Our church has faced many challenges over the past year.

Some problems remain unresolved. We are still waiting for answers in several areas, and the road ahead is not clear at every turn.

But gratitude does not mean that life is free from difficulty. Nor does it mean that every prayer has been answered in the way we hoped.

Scripture tells us:

> “Give thanks in all circumstances; for this is the will of God in Christ Jesus for you.”
>
> — 1 Thessalonians 5:18 (ESV)

True gratitude means recognizing the grace we have already received even while we are still waiting for what we do not yet have. It means trusting the Lord who leads us even when we cannot see the entire road ahead.

We may not yet have a church building of our own, but we already have a spiritual family whose members love one another.

A building provides a place for the church to gather. But the church itself is made up of people whose lives are being transformed by grace and who love one another in Christ.

Today, as I looked at the smiles of our brothers and sisters, the children among us, and the seven newly baptized believers, I was reminded again that the church's greatest treasure has never been its buildings. **Its greatest treasure is lives.**

## A Final Word

Thanksgiving Day will pass. The food on our tables will be eaten, and today's photographs will become memories.

But may the seeds of faith planted today continue to take root, sprout, grow, and bear fruit through the years.

May all seven who were baptized today, young and old alike, continue to know the Lord more deeply, experience his grace, and follow him throughout their lives.

And may our spiritual family, living far from the lands where many of us were born, continue to love, support, and build one another up. May more wandering lives find a place to belong here, and may more lonely hearts discover warmth and companionship.

We give thanks for the grace of the past, treasure the fellowship of today, and look toward the future with hope.

The greatest happiness in life is not possessing everything this world can offer. It is knowing the Lord of life and having people with whom we can walk, hand in hand, in love along life's long journey.

Thank you, God, for making us one family in a foreign land.

**Happy Thanksgiving!**

*November 26, 2026 · Indianapolis, Indiana, USA*
