Rev. Chongzhu Paul Zhang · Sunday, November 22, 2026 · Revelation 11:7–14

## Why Do Even the Faithful Fall?

Brothers and sisters, peace to you in the Lord. Last Sunday we saw two witnesses clothed in sackcloth, sent by God to speak in the darkness. As we read on today, they are lying dead in the street of the great city. People come from far and wide to look. They refuse even to let the bodies be buried. Some are so glad they send one another gifts.

Does a question rise in your heart as you read? Lord, you sent these two men. They bore witness just as you told them to. How could it end like this? John the Baptist was killed. Stephen was stoned. Paul went through one hardship after another. Had God forgotten his servants?

Brothers and sisters, keep reading. Read until the breath comes from God, until the two men stand on their feet, until you hear the voice from heaven say, “Come up hither” (Rev. 11:12, KJV). When we have read the whole passage, we see that the people in the street celebrated too soon.

## 1. The Witness Is Complete (Verses 7–10)

Pay attention to these words in verse 7: “when they shall have finished their testimony” (KJV). John tells us that they finished their testimony before he tells us that the beast from the bottomless pit made war against them and killed them. The beast could take their lives. The work God had entrusted to them was already done.

We tend to expect flowers and applause when the work is done. Your business grows, you buy a home, you invite friends to celebrate. These are things to thank God for. Yet it is easy to let other people's opinions in through the door too, until they are the ones grading our lives. Someone I grew up with has a better job. Their children seem to be doing better than mine. Before long, I feel as though I have failed at life. Brothers and sisters, if no one applauds today, will you still finish what God has given you to do?

When I taught in China, I used to tell the young people preparing to go out and preach the gospel to be ready. Some will be glad to hear what God has entrusted you to say. Others will be uncomfortable. We need to examine ourselves too. It is easy to mistake a quick temper for courage or to call every quarrel suffering for the Lord. What matters to him is whether we will obey him and bear the cost of that obedience. A business owner would rather lose an order than falsify it. Nobody else may ever know, but the Lord knows. He says, “be thou faithful unto death, and I will give thee a crown of life” (Rev. 2:10, KJV).

John says that the city is called Sodom and Egypt in a spiritual sense. It is also where their Lord was crucified. Sodom brings sin to mind, and Egypt brings bondage to mind. It is in the street of such a city that the two witnesses lie. People see them there, and some actually send gifts to one another. Why? The prophets' words made them uneasy. God's word showed them things they did not want to face. Dealing with the problem was too hard, so they set out to get rid of the people who had raised it.

We have some questions of our own to ask. If Scripture tells me to give up my greed, will I let it go? If it tells me to forgive someone who has wronged me, will I forgive? It is easy enough to count the sermons we have listened to and the preachers we have heard. The last time I heard God's word, did I do what it said?

We Chinese know how to keep accounts. In business we calculate costs. When we invest, we calculate returns. At home we count our expenses. But let me ask you something. Have you ever reckoned with the state of your soul? You may know exactly what you will earn this year and how you plan to spend your retirement. Can you calculate how many days you have left? When you stand before God, what answer will your ledger give for you? The Lord Jesus asks, “For what is a man profited, if he shall gain the whole world, and lose his own soul?” (Matt. 16:26, KJV). The people of the city thought that once they had silenced the two prophets, they could rest easy. God's word was still there.

## 2. Raised to Life (Verse 11)

Three and a half days pass. Scripture says, “the Spirit of life from God entered into them, and they stood upon their feet” (Rev. 11:11, KJV). The people who had come to watch are now afraid. The two men stand up in the very place where they had been lying. The breath that raises them comes from God.

This takes me back to Genesis, where God breathes into the man's nostrils and the man becomes a living soul (Gen. 2:7). It also takes me to the valley Ezekiel saw. The valley was full of dry bones, but God caused breath to enter them, and they stood up (Ezek. 37:1–14). Ezekiel's vision gave hope to Israel in its despair. In Revelation, the same God who gives life raises the two slain witnesses to their feet. Where we see the end of the road, God still has a way forward.

Three and a half days. Those were the days of the witnesses' humiliation, and those days came to an end. Brothers and sisters, perhaps you are waiting for news in a hospital corridor. Perhaps worry for your children keeps you awake. Perhaps you have served for years and are so tired you hardly know how to begin a prayer. This verse gives us no calendar telling us the day the illness will lift or the trouble will pass. It turns our eyes to the God who holds our lives in his hands. Even in the valley of the shadow of death, he is still with you.

Some of us grew up hearing that dying is like a lamp going out. But Scripture tells us that death comes to us all and that judgment follows (Heb. 9:27). Scripture also takes us to the tomb of Jesus. On the cross he said, “It is finished” (John 19:30, KJV). His body was laid in a tomb. All the disciples could see was the stone at its entrance. On the third day, the tomb was empty. The Lord who willingly laid down his life also had the power to take it up again (John 10:17–18). He rose from the dead, the firstfruits of those who have fallen asleep (1 Cor. 15:20).

So when Jesus says, “I am the resurrection, and the life: he that believeth in me, though he were dead, yet shall he live” (John 11:25, KJV), we can entrust both our life and our death to him. Even the grave cannot take us out of his hands.

## 3. Taken Up into Heaven (Verses 12–14)

A great voice from heaven says to the two prophets, “Come up hither.” They go up to heaven in a cloud. John makes a point of adding, “and their enemies beheld them” (Rev. 11:12, KJV).

Their enemies saw them lying in the street. Now their enemies see God take them up. Their humiliation was public. So was their vindication. The words “Come up hither” carry more weight than all the noise of the city. Brothers and sisters, someone who praises you today may forget you tomorrow. But one day you will stand before the Lord. Whose voice will you most want to hear then?

John goes on to tell us that the earth shook, a tenth of the city fell, and seven thousand people died. Those who remained were afraid and gave glory to the God of heaven. Verse 14 says the second woe has passed and the third is coming soon. The passage shifts our attention from what happened to the two prophets to what happened to the whole city. People may reject God's warning. His justice will still be made known.

Today we can still open the Bible, still hear the gospel, still confess our sins to God. All of that is grace. The sun comes up as usual and the days go by, so we assume repentance can wait. But who can hold tomorrow in their hands? God has let us hear his voice today. We can turn to him today.

## Who Are We Depending On?

At the beginning of Revelation, Jesus Christ is called “the faithful witness, and the first begotten of the dead” (Rev. 1:5, KJV). As we read what happens to the two witnesses, it is this faithful Lord we are meant to see. He gave his life for sinners, accomplished our salvation through the cross, and rose from the dead. Our deepest need is to know how sinners can stand before a holy God. Our possessions cannot confess our sins for us. Our achievements cannot ask for our pardon. The Lord Jesus himself bore our sins, and he himself calls us to turn to him and receive forgiveness and new life.

Brothers and sisters, if you have yet to trust the Lord, you can come to him today. If you already believe but are finding yourself more and more dependent on people's praise, come back to the cross today. If you are suffering and even one more step feels too hard, remember the moment the two witnesses stood up. Life comes from God. The Jesus you trust has risen.

Death is not the end of witness. May we depend on the risen Lord and faithfully finish what he has entrusted to us, until the day we see him face to face.

## Prayer of Response

Merciful and glorious Lord, we come before you and confess that we often give too much weight to money, position, and people's praise. We forget what pleases you and what you have entrusted to us. Forgive us and awaken our slumbering hearts. Help us depend on your Spirit and follow Jesus Christ closely.

Comfort those who mourn, strengthen those who have grown discouraged in serving you, and sustain our brothers and sisters in hardship. Help us hold on to the hope of resurrection in the face of death and remain faithful witnesses amid the temptations of this world. Revive our church. Teach us to take up our cross and follow you until we see you face to face. To you be all the glory. In the name of the Lord Jesus Christ we pray. Amen.
