# True Victory

**Scripture: Revelation 4:9--11 (KJV)**

> **Revelation 4:9** --- And when those beasts give glory and honour and
> thanks to him that sat on the throne, who liveth for ever and ever,\
> **Revelation 4:10** --- The four and twenty elders fall down before
> him that sat on the throne, and worship him that liveth for ever and
> ever, and cast their crowns before the throne, saying,\
> **Revelation 4:11** --- Thou art worthy, O Lord, to receive glory and
> honour and power: for thou hast created all things, and for thy
> pleasure they are and were created.

**Big Idea: True victory is bowing down and giving the glory back to
God.**

## Introduction: We Are All Wearing Crowns

Brothers and sisters, we often feel exhausted. We assume it is because
we have too much to do or because life is too difficult. But Scripture
shines a light on something deeper: sometimes we are tired not simply
because our responsibilities are heavy, but because the "crowns" on our
heads are heavy.

Those crowns may be called reputation, achievement that cannot afford to
lose, absolute control over our families, or even the burden of having
to appear spiritual. We want to prove ourselves, maintain our image,
win, and be noticed, so we hold tightly to those crowns.

But Revelation 4 gives us a breathtaking picture. The most honored
elders do not try to keep their crowns securely on their heads. They lay
them down.

True worship does not crown the self; it gives glory to God. True
victory is not measured by how high I can stand, but by how low I am
willing to bow before the throne.

## I. The Starting Point of Victory: See the Throne and Give Glory to God (4:9)

The four living creatures give "glory and honour and thanks to him that
sat on the throne, who liveth for ever and ever."

The first movement of worship is not, "What can I do?" It is, "Who is
worthy?" The living creatures give glory, honor, and thanks to the One
seated on the throne.

Revelation repeatedly reminds us through the language of worship that
God is worthy. He is worthy not merely because we need Him to solve our
problems, but because He is God. The Psalms follow the same pattern:
"The heavens declare the glory of God" (see Ps. 19:1). Glory does not
originate in us. It originates in God.

Christians have proposed different symbolic meanings for the specific
appearance of the four living creatures. The text itself, however, does
not assign a particular human virtue to the lion, calf, man, and flying
eagle. We therefore do not need to press the imagery beyond what
Scripture says. The central picture is clear: these majestic created
beings are not competing with one another for glory. Together they turn
toward the throne and give glory to God.

Something remarkable happens when our attention shifts from "the crown
on my head" to "the throne in heaven." When you stop treating yourself
as the center of the universe, anxiety begins to loosen its grip. You
remember that the meaning of the world and the ultimate weight of your
life were never yours to carry. The One on the throne is holding them.

**Victory begins by seeing the throne.**

And when glory is truly given to God, the natural response is to bow
down.

## II. The Action of Victory: Fall Down in Worship and Cast the Crown Before the Throne (4:10)

"The four and twenty elders fall down before him that sat on the throne
... and cast their crowns before the throne."

Two actions follow one another:

1.  They fall down in worship.
2.  They cast down their crowns.

This is a profoundly self-denying picture. I no longer allow my crown to
define me. I return it to God.

Christians have understood the identity of the twenty-four elders in
different ways. One common interpretation sees them as representing the
fullness of God's redeemed people. Whatever interpretation we adopt,
their action in the text is unmistakable: they possess crowns, and yet
they cast those crowns before the throne.

Here is the paradox of heavenly victory: victory is not measured by how
high we can stand, but by how low we are willing to bow before the
throne.

The crowns are associated with identity, authority, honor, and victory.
The elders are not crownless. They simply know where their crowns came
from and where the glory ultimately belongs.

Laying down the crown does not mean denying the gifts God has given us
or pretending that His work in our lives does not matter. It means
confessing:

**Every gift is not my possession but God's trust; every fruit is not
proof of my greatness but evidence of God's grace.**

Paul asks:

> "What hast thou that thou didst not receive? now if thou didst receive
> it, why dost thou glory?" (see 1 Cor. 4:7, KJV)

### How Do We Wear Crowns Today?

Our crowns today are often more subtle.

Social media trains us to manage an image. A culture of success and
comparison trains us to prove ourselves. Even in the church, we can wear
crowns: ministry can become the source of our self-worth, a ministry can
become something we possess, and a title can become a position of
status.

The problem is not service, effort, or achievement itself. The question
is whether these things have displaced God and become tools by which we
prove ourselves.

Revelation 4 tells us that true victory is not holding these things more
tightly. It is being willing to lay them down.

Why is God worthy of our crowns?

The elders give us the answer: He is the Creator.

## III. The Foundation of Victory: The Lord Is Worthy Because He Created All Things (4:11)

"Thou art worthy, O Lord, to receive glory and honour and power: for
thou hast created all things."

The elders declare that God is worthy, and they give the reason:

**He created all things.**

Revelation 4 emphasizes the Creator, while Revelation 5 moves our
attention further toward the Lamb and His redeeming work. That narrative
sequence reminds us of a foundational truth: before we speak about "I
have been saved" or "I have received grace," we first confront an even
more basic reality---God is Lord, and we are creatures.

If God is the Creator, then:

-   life is not an accident;
-   I am not my own master;
-   everything I have has purpose and belonging under God.

The text says that all things exist according to His will and pleasure.
That changes the most basic question of life. Instead of asking only,
"What do I want?" we begin to ask:

**"Lord, what is Your will?"**

Some of our endless "Why?" questions expose how anxious we become when
we cannot control everything. But when we return to the Creator and
confess that our lives and this world ultimately belong to Him, our
hearts can begin to rest again.

True victory is not having more. It is returning to the right center:

-   not "I am worthy," but "the Lord is worthy";
-   not "I am in control," but "the Lord reigns";
-   not "I must prove myself," but "I will glorify God."

The world often tells us that victory means defeating others, climbing
higher, and holding on to more.

Revelation gives us a very different picture:

**Victory means bowing before the throne, laying down the crown, and
giving the glory to God.**

## Conclusion: Lay Down the Crown

Brothers and sisters, as we leave this passage and return to everyday
life, I invite you to become quiet before God and honestly ask three
questions.

### First: What am I most unwilling to lay down?

Is it my reputation?\
My desire for control?\
The pressure to appear successful?\
Or the "spiritual image" I carefully maintain before other people?

Ask the Holy Spirit to shine His light on us so that we can recognize
the heavy false crown we have been wearing.

### Second: Is the crown I am holding so tightly actually making me more like Christ?

Or is it simply making me more exhausted, more proud, and more afraid
every day that I might lose it?

### Third: Am I willing, right now, to lay this crown before the throne of the Lord?

When we are willing to ask these questions, the idols of our hearts
become clearer.

We can pray together:

> Lord, this crown does not belong to me.\
> I will no longer crown myself.\
> May all honor and glory belong to You.\
> Teach me so that my life will no longer revolve around myself, but
> will be centered on You alone.

True victory is bowing down and giving the glory back to God.

May we all receive this true victory.

Amen.
