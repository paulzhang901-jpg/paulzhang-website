# When the World Is Shaken

## Scripture: Revelation 8:7–12

## Central Message

God shakes the created world on which people depend so that we may see that no created thing can provide our ultimate security. He calls us, while judgment has not yet come in its fullness, to turn back to the Kingdom that cannot be shaken.

## Core Sentence

God shakes what we depend on so that we will seek again the Kingdom that cannot be shaken.

## Scripture Reading

**Revelation 8:7 (KJV)** — The first angel sounded, and there followed hail and fire mingled with blood, and they were cast upon the earth: and the third part of trees was burnt up, and all green grass was burnt up.

**Revelation 8:8 (KJV)** — And the second angel sounded, and as it were a great mountain burning with fire was cast into the sea: and the third part of the sea became blood;

**Revelation 8:9 (KJV)** — And the third part of the creatures which were in the sea, and had life, died; and the third part of the ships were destroyed.

**Revelation 8:10 (KJV)** — And the third angel sounded, and there fell a great star from heaven, burning as it were a lamp, and it fell upon the third part of the rivers, and upon the fountains of waters;

**Revelation 8:11 (KJV)** — And the name of the star is called Wormwood: and the third part of the waters became wormwood; and many men died of the waters, because they were made bitter.

**Revelation 8:12 (KJV)** — And the fourth angel sounded, and the third part of the sun was smitten, and the third part of the moon, and the third part of the stars; so as the third part of them was darkened, and the day shone not for a third part of it, and the night likewise.
## Introduction: If One Day Everything You Depend On Begins to Shake

Every one of us needs security.

The land gives us homes and food; the sea brings transportation and trade; rivers and springs sustain life; the sun, moon, and stars give light and order.

These are all good parts of God's creation. The problem is not that we use them. The problem is that we slowly begin to depend on them more than we depend on the God who created them.

If we have money, we feel safe. If we are healthy, the future seems reliable. If our work is stable, life seems secure. If our nation is strong, we assume nothing will change.

Revelation 8 presents a shocking picture: the earth is struck, the sea is struck, the waters become bitter, and the light grows dark.

This is not meant to satisfy our curiosity about end-time disasters. It asks a deeper question:

> **If one day everything on which you depend for life is shaken, what will remain?**

In the previous message, Revelation 8:1–6 showed us heaven's silence, the prayers of the saints rising like incense before the throne, and the fire from the altar being thrown down upon the earth.

Now the trumpets begin to sound.

The first four trumpets fall upon the earth, the sea, the rivers and springs, and the sun, moon, and stars.

Some may ask: Is this nuclear war? Is it an asteroid? Is “Wormwood” a particular nuclear accident? These questions may be discussed, but they must not become the center of the sermon.

John first shows us a theological picture: God is shaking the created world on which people depend.

## I. The Earth Is Struck — The Created World Is Not Eternal

### Revelation 8:7

The first angel blows his trumpet, and hail and fire mixed with blood are thrown upon the earth. The earth, the trees, and the grass are struck.

This clearly reminds us of the ten plagues in Exodus. In Exodus 9, hail and fire came upon Egypt; Revelation again uses similar language of judgment.

This told the church suffering under Rome that the God who once judged Pharaoh and delivered Israel is still Lord of history.

Pharaoh is not God. Rome is not God. No empire is God. No civilization can be humanity's ultimate security.

### 1. From Creation to the Shaking of the Created Order

Genesis 1 shows us God creating the heavens and the earth, plants, the sea, and the lights of heaven, declaring what He made to be “very good.”

Revelation 8 shows the earth damaged, the sea damaged, the waters polluted, and the light diminished.

This may be seen as a picture of “un-creation,” or of the created order being dismantled. The point is not that Revelation 8 mechanically reverses the exact order of Genesis 1. The point is that the created world God originally gave humanity as a home is now shaken in judgment.

Why? Because people enjoy creation while rejecting the Creator. They receive the gifts while refusing the Giver. They even turn created things into idols.

Romans 1 describes this reversal: people “worshiped and served the creature rather than the Creator.” Revelation 8 seems to let us see the final result of that reversal: when creation is treated as God, it cannot finally carry God's place.

### 2. The First Four Seals and the First Four Trumpets

We can also make a simple comparison with Revelation 6.

The first four seals show war, famine, and death—the order of human society being shaken.

The first four trumpets show the earth, sea, waters, and light—the created environment on which humanity depends being shaken.

We should not separate the two too absolutely, because both seals and trumpets remain under God's sovereignty. But overall we can see this: the world within us will be shaken, and the world outside us will be shaken. Neither society nor nature can be our god.

So the first trumpet asks us: Where exactly have you built your life?

## II. The Waters Turn Bitter — Creation Cannot Satisfy Us Apart from the Source of Life

### Revelation 8:8–11

The second trumpet falls upon the sea. Something like a great mountain burning with fire is thrown into it. A third of the sea becomes blood, and the living creatures and ships in the sea are destroyed.

The third trumpet falls upon the rivers and springs. A great star falls, and its name is Wormwood. The waters become bitter.

### 1. Wormwood Represents Bitterness and Judgment

In the Old Testament, wormwood is often associated with the bitter fruit of sin and God's judgment. Jeremiah 9:15 says, “I will feed this people with wormwood and give them poisonous water to drink.”

The shocking point is that water, which should give life, becomes bitter. What should satisfy now harms.

Is this not also a picture of sin?

The world constantly tells us, “A little more, and you will be satisfied.” A little more money. A little more success. A little more pleasure. A little more status. A little more control.

But people often discover that the more they gain, the sweeter life is not necessarily within them.

Wealth is originally a gift, but it can become anxiety. Success can be something for which we thank God, but it can become pride and bondage. Relationships are blessings, but they can become idols we believe we cannot live without. Pleasure has its proper place, but it can never fill the soul.

Why? Because created things can never replace the Creator.

If we leave the source of life, we may drink all the waters of the world and still remain thirsty.

Paul writes in 1 Timothy 6:6–10:

“Godliness with contentment is great gain, for we brought nothing into the world, and we cannot take anything out of the world. But if we have food and clothing, with these we will be content. But those who desire to be rich fall into temptation, into a snare, into many senseless and harmful desires that plunge people into ruin and destruction. For the love of money is a root of all kinds of evils. It is through this craving that some have wandered away from the faith and pierced themselves with many pangs.”

### 2. From Bitter Water to the Gospel

There is also a beautiful contrast in Scripture.

In Exodus 15, Israel came to Marah because the water was bitter and could not be drunk. God later made the bitter water sweet.

In the New Testament, Jesus declares, “If anyone thirsts, let him come to me and drink” (John 7:37). He also said to the Samaritan woman, “Whoever drinks of the water that I will give him will never be thirsty again” (John 4:14).

At the cross, Jesus Himself bore the bitter cup of sin and judgment.

Therefore the gospel is not, first of all, “Work hard so that you do not drink the world's bitter water.” The gospel is that Christ has already borne the judgment sinners deserved, so that we may freely come to the fountain of life.

That is the final direction of Revelation. In Revelation 21:6 God says, “I will give from the spring of the water of life without payment to the thirsty.” Revelation 22 again presents the river of the water of life.

So the bitter water of Revelation 8 is not the final picture of the Bible. The final picture is the water of life flowing from the throne of God and of the Lamb.

The question is: Where are you trying to quench your thirst today—in the world, or in Christ?

## III. The Lights Are Struck — God Shakes What Can Be Shaken to Reveal What Cannot

### Revelation 8:12

The fourth trumpet sounds, and a third of the sun, moon, and stars is struck. The light becomes dark.

This again reminds us of the plague of darkness in Exodus 10. It also recalls the cosmic language used by the Old Testament prophets when they spoke of the day of the Lord.

### 1. Even the Most Stable Things Will Shake

When human beings look at the sun, we assume it will rise tomorrow. The moon will appear. The stars will still be there. They seem to represent order, stability, and reliability.

But Revelation says that even they are not ultimate.

Why? Because the sun is not God. The universe is not God. The laws of nature are not God. Civilization is not God. Humanity is not the master of the universe.

### 2. An Important Number: “One-Third”

The first four trumpets repeatedly mention one-third. Not everything is destroyed. This is very important.

The judgment is real and severe, but it is still limited. This shows that God has not lost control. The trumpets are not yet the final judgment.

Therefore the trumpets are not only judgment; they are also warning. God is still giving people an opportunity to wake up, turn back, and repent—to stop treating creation as the Creator.

By chapter 9 we see a tragic result: even after these warnings, people “still did not repent” (Revelation 9:20–21).

So the truly frightening thing is not only disaster. The truly frightening thing is that God has shaken our world, yet our hearts still have not been awakened.

Why does God shake things? Hebrews 12:26–28 gives us a very good explanation: God will “shake the heavens and the earth,” in order “that the things that cannot be shaken may remain.” Then it says, “Therefore, since we are receiving a kingdom that cannot be shaken, let us be grateful.”

That is today's message. God shakes what can be shaken so that we will hold again to what cannot be shaken.

## Conclusion: Shaking Is Not the Final Picture

Brothers and sisters, perhaps your world is shaking today.

The economy can shake. Work can shake. Your body can shake. Your family can shake. Society can shake. Nations can shake. Even things we once thought unbreakable can change.

Revelation does not tell us that Christians will never lose anything. It tells us that even when everything that can be shaken is shaken, we still possess a kingdom that cannot be shaken.

The earth will shake, but God's throne will not shake.

The waters may become bitter, but Christ remains the fountain of living water.

The sun and moon may grow dark, but the glory of the Lamb will never go out.

The world will pass away, but God's kingdom will endure forever.

Most importantly, God still says “one-third.” The warning is still sounding. The door of grace has not yet completely closed.

So today's most important question is not, “When exactly will the end come?” It is, “The trumpet has sounded its warning—am I willing to repent?”

It is not, “What exactly is this star?” It is, “What exactly is my life built upon?”

If my security is built on money, return to God. If it is built on health, return to God. If it is built on success, career, family, nation, or any created thing, put it back in its proper place.

Creation may be enjoyed, but it must not be worshiped. Gifts may be received with thanksgiving, but they must not replace the Giver.

So when the world is shaken, do not ask only, “What is happening?” Ask also, “Lord, what do You want me to see?” Then anchor your life again in Christ.

Because God shakes what we depend on so that we will seek again the Kingdom that cannot be shaken.

The first four trumpets ultimately do not mean that our eyes should be locked on disaster. They lift our eyes from a world that can be shaken and raise them again toward the God and the Lamb seated on the throne.

The world will shake, but the Lamb still reigns.

Amen.
