# True Worship

## Holy! Holy! Holy! --- Recovering Reverence in a Noisy World

**Scripture: Revelation 4:4--8 (KJV)**

> **Revelation 4:4** --- And round about the throne were four and twenty
> seats: and upon the seats I saw four and twenty elders sitting,
> clothed in white raiment; and they had on their heads crowns of gold.\
> **Revelation 4:5** --- And out of the throne proceeded lightnings and
> thunderings and voices: and there were seven lamps of fire burning
> before the throne, which are the seven Spirits of God.\
> **Revelation 4:6** --- And before the throne there was a sea of glass
> like unto crystal: and in the midst of the throne, and round about the
> throne, were four beasts full of eyes before and behind.\
> **Revelation 4:7** --- And the first beast was like a lion, and the
> second beast like a calf, and the third beast had a face as a man, and
> the fourth beast was like a flying eagle.\
> **Revelation 4:8** --- And the four beasts had each of them six wings
> about him; and they were full of eyes within: and they rest not day
> and night, saying, Holy, holy, holy, Lord God Almighty, which was, and
> is, and is to come.

**Big Idea: Worship is not an atmosphere; it is our response to the
holiness of God.**

## Introduction: We Do Not Lack Music; We Lack Reverence

The church today does not lack music, programs, or well-planned
services. But there is something we often lack: reverence.

It is easy for us to evaluate worship by asking, "Was I moved? Was I
comforted? Did I like it?" When that happens, worship can be reduced to
an experience. But biblical worship begins not with "What did I get?"
but with "Who is God?"

Revelation 4 brings the church back to the center of worship: the throne
is central, holiness is central, and God's worthiness is central.

## I. The Order of Worship --- The Community Around the Throne (4:4)

"And round about the throne were four and twenty seats: and upon the
seats I saw four and twenty elders sitting, clothed in white raiment;
and they had on their heads crowns of gold."

### 1. The Twenty-Four Elders: Worship Is Not a Performance by a Few

Christians have understood the identity of the twenty-four elders in
different ways. One common interpretation sees them as symbolizing the
fullness of God's redeemed people, calling to mind the twelve tribes of
Israel and the twelve apostles as representatives of God's people.
Whatever specific interpretation we adopt, the vision clearly tells us
this much: heavenly worship is centered on the throne and is the
corporate response of God's people, not a performance by a select few.

### 2. White Garments and Crowns: Worshipers Stand by Grace

In Revelation, white garments are often associated with cleansing,
victory, and acceptance before God (see Rev. 3:5). The crowns remind us
that although God's people may appear weak on earth, their identity and
honor are given and confirmed by God.

Worship is not about making ourselves look more spiritual. It is about
confessing before God, "I stand here by grace."

As Revelation 4 continues, verse 10 shows the twenty-four elders laying
their crowns before the throne. The picture is striking. Those who wear
crowns in heaven do not cling to their glory when they come before God;
they return the glory to the One who gave it.

Yet we often carry the world's "crowns" into worship---titles,
achievements, reputation, and the desire to control. True worship
teaches us again: the throne belongs to God, and every crown ultimately
belongs to Him.

**Key truth: True worship has a God-given order---the throne is the
center, God's people respond together, and holiness marks the scene.**

When we are tempted to reduce worship to whether the music sounds good,
Scripture brings us before the throne and asks us first to see whose
house this is and who is reigning.

## II. The Atmosphere of Worship --- The Trembling of God's Presence and the Fire of the Spirit (4:5--6)

"And out of the throne proceeded lightnings and thunderings and voices
... and there were seven lamps of fire burning before the throne ... and
before the throne there was a sea of glass like unto crystal."

### 1. Lightning and Thunder: Reverence Before the Holy Presence of God

Lightning, voices, and thunder proceed from the throne. The scene
recalls God's descent upon Mount Sinai when He gave the law (Ex.
19:16--20). When God truly makes His presence known, human beings cannot
remain casual.

The point is not to manufacture fear. It is to recover reverence. God is
not an object for us to use; He is the Lord whom we worship.

### 2. Seven Lamps of Fire: The Fullness of the Spirit's Work

The text explicitly identifies the seven lamps of fire as "the seven
Spirits of God." This expression appears repeatedly in Revelation
(Rev. 1:4; 4:5; 5:6) and communicates the fullness and completeness of
the Holy Spirit's work.

Worship is not something we manufacture by building an atmosphere. It
requires the work of the Spirit. Without the Spirit, worship can become
mere performance. When the Spirit works, He brings genuine illumination,
renewal, and a turning of our hearts toward God.

### 3. The Sea of Glass: Seeing Ultimate Order Before the Throne

Before the throne is "a sea of glass like unto crystal." In biblical and
ancient Near Eastern imagery, the sea can at times be associated with
chaos and threat. Therefore, the tranquil, glass-like sea can help us
see an important theological picture: the storms of this world have not
exceeded the sovereignty of the One on the throne.

This does not mean that Revelation 4:6 explicitly defines the sea of
glass as "conquered chaos." Rather, the image resonates with the
chapter's larger vision of the throne and God's sovereign rule.

Worship, then, is not an escape from the storm. It is bringing the storm
before the throne and allowing our hearts to be recalibrated by the
sovereignty of God.

First, see reality clearly: there is a throne at the center of the
universe; chaos does not have the final word.\
Second, see ourselves clearly: before the holy God, no creature can
stand by pretending.\
Third, see the Lord alone: our attention turns away from ourselves
toward Him, and we cry, "Holy!"

**Key truth: Worship does not merely make us louder; it makes us
clearer. In the holiness of God and the work of the Spirit, our hearts
are brought back into alignment.**

## III. The Content of Worship --- "Holy" Without Ceasing (4:6--8)

"The four beasts ... rest not day and night, saying, Holy, holy, holy,
Lord God Almighty, which was, and is, and is to come."

### 1. The Four Living Creatures: Creation Ministers Before the Throne

The vision of the four living creatures has clear connections with the
living creatures in Ezekiel 1. Revelation portrays them with features
like a lion, a calf, a man, and a flying eagle. They are full of eyes
and each has six wings.

We should not press the symbolism of every feature beyond what the text
itself gives us. But the overall picture is unmistakable: these majestic
created beings are not the center of worship. They too stand before the
throne and praise God day and night.

Their many eyes also reinforce imagery of wakefulness, watchfulness, and
perception. Before God there is no room for false appearances. True
worship brings us into His light.

### 2. "Holy, Holy, Holy": Worship Begins by Declaring Who God Is

The threefold cry of "Holy" immediately recalls Isaiah's vision of the
Lord in the temple: "Holy, holy, holy" (Isa. 6:3).

God's holiness is not a minor idea that can be pushed to the edge of our
faith. His love is holy love. His justice is holy justice. His mercy is
holy mercy.

Therefore, worship does not begin by asking, "What can God do for me?"
It begins by confessing, "Who is God?"

### 3. "Lord God Almighty, Which Was, and Is, and Is to Come": Returning Time to God's Sovereignty

When anxiety about tomorrow pulls at our hearts, worship declares that
God rules history. Revelation does not first give the church a timetable
by which we can control the future. It first places the church before
the eternal Lord.

This is the stability worship gives us---not because we know everything
that will happen tomorrow, but because we know who sits on the throne.

## IV. The Holy Throne and the Grace of the Lamb

When we see the lightning, thunder, and holiness surrounding the throne,
we must also ask: How can sinners like us come before a holy God?

Revelation 5 will bring the Lamb clearly into view, and later visions in
Revelation further connect white garments with the Lamb's redeeming
work. We are able to come before God ultimately not because we are
respectable, and not because we performed well this week, but because of
the salvation God has given us in Christ.

True worship, therefore, is not the creation of a warm atmosphere that
numbs us to reality. It is redeemed sinners bowing before the holy God
and surrendering the "crowns" we fight so hard to keep---our
achievements, our reputation, our desire for control---and saying:

"Lord, holy, holy, holy! You are Lord; I am not."

True reverence means being overwhelmed by His holiness and, at the same
time, melted by His grace.

## Contemporary Application: Recovering Reverence in a Noisy World

### 1. Restore Worship from "Experience" to "Response"

Ask yourself: Did I come to worship only to receive a certain feeling,
or did I come to give God the glory He deserves?

### 2. Let Holiness Lead to Repentance and Healing

When Isaiah saw the Lord, he first confessed his sin (Isa. 6:5), and
then he experienced cleansing and commissioning (Isa. 6:7--8).

We too can practice repentance in worship. This is not about remaining
trapped in self-condemnation. It is about allowing God's light to enter
our lives and returning our hearts to Him.

### 3. Prepare Before Sunday Worship: Let the Throne Recalibrate Your Heart

On the day before worship, read Revelation 4:8.

Before entering worship, pray:

"Lord, let me reverence You before I speak of my needs. Let me see Your
holiness before I ask for Your grace."

## Conclusion: Revival in Worship Begins with "Holy"

When the church learns again to say, "Holy," it will learn again to
reverence God. When we recover reverence, we will learn again to repent.
When we learn again to repent, we will experience again the cleansing
and commissioning of God.

May the Lord use Revelation 4 to bring our worship out of the noise and
back before the throne:

**Holy! Holy! Holy!**

Amen.
