**Scripture: 1 Samuel 17**

## Two Armies Face Off

1 Samuel 17:1–3

David and Goliath: Faith Over Fear. This story comes from First Samuel, chapter seventeen. The Philistines gathered to fight Israel. Saul and his army camped in the Valley of Elah. The two armies stood on opposite hills, with a valley between them. They were ready for battle, but a champion from the Philistine camp brought Israel to a standstill. In the face of a threat that seemed impossible to overcome, Scripture shows us a young man who trusted the living God.

## The Giant's Challenge

1 Samuel 17:4–11, 16

The champion was Goliath, from Gath. He wore a bronze helmet and heavy armor. He carried a spear, and a shield bearer walked ahead of him. He challenged Israel to choose one man to fight him, staking the fate of the two armies on a single contest. Saul and the Israelites were terrified. For forty days, morning and evening, Goliath came forward to challenge them. Scripture does not hide their fear. Even a well equipped army can become paralyzed when it looks only at the danger before it.

## David Arrives

1 Samuel 17:12–32

Meanwhile, David was tending his father's sheep. His three oldest brothers had gone to war with Saul. His father, Jesse, sent him with food to visit them. David left the sheep with a keeper and set out early. At the battlefield, he heard Goliath's challenge and saw the Israelites retreat. What troubled him was more than the enemy's strength: this man was defying the army of the living God. David's words reached Saul. Brought before the king, David offered to fight the Philistine.

## A Declaration of Faith

1 Samuel 17:33–47

Saul thought David was too young to face an experienced warrior. But David remembered how the Lord had delivered him from lions and bears while he protected the sheep. He trusted that same Lord to rescue him now. David tried Saul's armor, then took it off because he was not used to it. He chose five smooth stones from a stream, put them in his shepherd's bag, and approached with his staff and sling. Goliath mocked him. David declared that he came in the name of the Lord Almighty. The outcome belonged to the Lord.

## One Stone

1 Samuel 17:48–52

As Goliath advanced, David ran toward him. He took a stone from his bag and slung it. The stone struck Goliath's forehead, and he fell facedown. Scripture says David defeated the Philistine with a sling and a stone, without a sword in his hand. David then used Goliath's own sword to finish the battle. We will not show graphic violence. When the Philistines saw their champion was dead, they fled. The Israelites shouted and pursued them. The turning point was not superior equipment. It was God's deliverance.

## Where Victory Comes From

Reflection · 1 Samuel 17:47

That is the biblical account. Now consider its meaning. In First Samuel seventeen, verse forty seven, David declares that the Lord does not save by sword or spear. In the words of the King James Version, the battle is the Lord's. David did not deny the danger or simply wait. He acted courageously, using a familiar sling, while placing his hope in God. This story reveals God's glory and faithfulness. It is more than a lesson in self confidence. True victory rests on trust in the living God, not human strength.

## Living by Faith Today

Application · Distinct from the biblical narrative

What follows is an application for our lives, not another event in the story. When we face pressure, illness, or an uncertain future, we can bring our fears to God, seek him in prayer, and faithfully carry out today's responsibilities. Trust does not mean every problem will be resolved on our schedule, or every struggle will end as we expect. Our confidence rests in God himself. When fear rises, remember the truth David proclaimed. As we act, may we humbly use the abilities God has given us. May faith overcome fear, and may the living God receive the glory.
