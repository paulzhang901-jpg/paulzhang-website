# When Heaven Falls Silent

**Big Idea: When heaven seems silent, God has not left. He hears the
prayers of His people and includes the prayers of the saints within His
righteous governance of history.**

**Core Sentence: Heaven may be silent for a time, but God never stops
listening.**

**One Picture to Remember: A censer of incense rising before the
throne.**

## Introduction: If You Have Been Praying for a Long Time

Brothers and sisters, I want to begin with a promise for those who are
waiting.

If you have prayed about something for a long time and still have not
seen an answer, I hope these six verses will help you keep praying.

Sometimes the hardest thing in prayer is not hearing God say "No."

It is when God seems to say nothing.

You pray for your child, but he still has not returned to God.\
You pray for a family member to believe, and one year, five years, or
ten years pass with no visible change.\
You pray for your body, but the illness remains.\
You pray for your marriage, but the relationship remains tense.\
You pray for justice, but injustice seems to grow stronger.

Then we begin to ask:

"Lord, did You hear?"\
"Lord, how much longer?"\
"Lord, why are You not speaking?"\
Even, "Lord, where are You?"

Revelation 8 speaks directly into that experience.

In chapter 6, the saints beneath the altar have already cried:

> "How long, O Lord, holy and true...?" (Rev. 6:10, KJV)

They prayed, but the answer did not immediately arrive.

In chapter 7, God first shows the final outcome: He seals His people;
the nations stand before the throne; the Lamb shepherds them; God wipes
away every tear.

Then, in chapter 8, the Lamb opens the seventh seal.

We expect thunder at once.

Instead:

> "And when he had opened the seventh seal, there was silence in heaven
> about the space of half an hour." (Rev. 8:1, KJV)

Heaven suddenly becomes quiet.

And in that silence, John sees the prayers of the saints rising like
incense before God.

Remember one picture today:

**a censer of incense rising.**

When you feel that your prayer has not even passed through the ceiling,
Revelation pulls back heaven and shows you that it has come before the
throne.

## I. Silence in Heaven --- God's Silence Does Not Mean God's Absence (8:1--2)

The silence is striking because heaven has been full of sound.

In Revelation 4, the living creatures cry day and night, "Holy, holy,
holy."

In Revelation 5, countless angels proclaim the worthiness of the slain
Lamb.

In Revelation 7, the innumerable multitude cries that salvation belongs
to God and to the Lamb.

Then Revelation 8 says:

Silence.

### 1. This Is Not God's Absence; Heaven Stands in Awe Before Him

In the Old Testament, silence can appear in contexts of God's presence
and judgment.

Habakkuk 2:20 says:

> "But the LORD is in his holy temple: let all the earth keep silence
> before him." (KJV)

Zephaniah 1:7 likewise calls people to silence before the Lord in view
of the day of the Lord.

So the silence in Revelation 8 does not mean, "God has left."

Quite the opposite.

The Lamb is still there.\
The throne is still there.\
The seven angels still stand before God.\
History has not lost its Lord.

### 2. Immediately after the Silence, the Prayers of the Saints Appear

There is a powerful literary sequence:

verse 1: silence in heaven;\
verses 3--4: the prayers of the saints rise before God.

The text does not explicitly say that heaven became silent *in order to
listen* to the saints' prayers, so we should not claim more than
Scripture says.

But the sequence is still deeply moving.

After the great voices of heaven pause, the next thing John draws our
attention to is not another angelic chorus.

It is the prayers of God's people.

At the throne of God, the prayers of His people are not insignificant
sounds.

### 3. No Answer Yet Does Not Mean God Has Not Heard

We easily translate:

"I have not received an answer"

into:

"God did not hear."

We translate:

"My circumstances have not changed"

into:

"God is not working."

We translate:

"I have waited a long time"

into:

"God has forgotten me."

Revelation gives us another perspective.

Heaven may be silent, but the throne is still there.

When God is silent, do not mistake silence for absence.

When you wait, do not mistake waiting for forgetfulness.

When you cannot see, do not assume God is not at work.

**God's silence does not mean God's absence.**

## II. Incense Rises Before the Throne --- Not One Prayer Is Forgotten (8:3--4)

John sees an angel with a golden censer standing at the altar:

> "And there was given unto him much incense, that he should offer it
> with the prayers of all saints upon the golden altar which was before
> the throne." (Rev. 8:3, KJV)

Then:

> "And the smoke of the incense, which came with the prayers of the
> saints, ascended up before God." (Rev. 8:4, KJV)

Notice: the prayers of **all saints**.

Not merely famous Christians.\
Not only unusually spiritual people.

### 1. Incense Is an Important Biblical Image for Prayer

Psalm 141:2 says:

> "Let my prayer be set forth before thee as incense." (KJV)

Revelation 5:8 has already connected golden vessels of incense with the
prayers of the saints.

Now the image appears again.

Incense rises.

The prayers of the saints rise before God.

### 2. The Smallest Prayer on Earth Can Reach the Highest Throne in Heaven

This is one of the most comforting truths in the passage.

An ordinary believer prays in a bedroom. No one else hears.

A mother weeps for her child at midnight. No one else sees.

A patient whispers from a hospital bed, "Lord, help me." The voice is
weak.

A suffering believer may not even know what to say.

To the world, these voices are tiny.

But Revelation opens heaven and tells us:

**The smallest prayer on earth can come before the highest throne in
heaven.**

God knows your tears.\
God knows your sighs.\
God knows even what you cannot put into words.

### 3. Stop Saying, "All I Can Do Is Pray"

We often say:

"There is nothing else I can do. All I can do is pray."

That makes prayer sound like the last option after every useful option
has failed.

Revelation changes the picture.

Prayer is not:

"I have no options left."

Prayer is:

"I am bringing this matter before the highest throne in the universe."

Prayer does not control God. My prayer does not obligate Him to act
according to my method or my timetable.

But the one who prays knows:

This matter has been brought before God.

So instead of saying, "I can only pray," say:

**"I am going to bring this before the throne."**

### Application

When anxiety returns, turn it into prayer.

Bring your child before the throne.\
Bring your marriage before the throne.\
Bring your illness before the throne.\
Bring the church before the throne.\
Bring the suffering of the world before the throne.

The incense is rising.

## III. Fire Falls from the Altar --- Prayer Is Included in God's Righteous Governance of History (8:5--6)

The movement suddenly reverses.

First:

incense rises.

Then:

fire descends.

> "And the angel took the censer, and filled it with fire of the altar,
> and cast it into the earth." (Rev. 8:5, KJV)

Thunder, voices, lightning, and an earthquake follow. Then the seven
angels prepare to sound their trumpets.

The movement is striking:

Prayer rises before God.\
God's action comes upon the earth.

### 1. It Is Fire from the Altar

The text says the fire comes from the altar.

In Scripture the altar is bound up with worship, sacrifice, holiness,
and judgment.

In Revelation 6, the martyrs cry beneath the altar, "How long?"

In Revelation 8, the altar appears again.

The fire is therefore not a picture of God losing His temper, nor is it
human revenge receiving divine approval.

It is a picture of righteous action proceeding from the presence of the
holy God.

And Revelation is centered on the Lamb. The One who reigns is the Lamb
who was slain.

Therefore judgment in Revelation cannot be separated from the Lamb---the
Lord who bore sin, shed His blood, and called sinners to repentance.

God's justice is never arbitrary revenge.

### 2. God Answers Prayer, but Prayer Does Not Control God

This must be clear.

Revelation 8 does not teach:

"If we pray enough, God must immediately do exactly what we ask."

The saints in Revelation 6 already asked, "How long?" They experienced
waiting.

True faith is not:

"I prayed, therefore God must follow my plan."

True faith says:

"I pray because I believe He hears.\
I wait because I believe He sits on the throne."

God answers according to His time, wisdom, holiness, justice, and
complete redemptive purpose.

### 3. The Prayers of the Saints Appear in the Scene Where God Moves History Forward

Revelation 8 is bigger than the promise that God will help us with
personal difficulties, though our personal concerns certainly belong in
prayer.

After the prayers of the saints rise, the seven angels prepare to sound
their trumpets.

Within the vision, the prayers of the saints are placed in the very
scene where God's purposes in history move toward their appointed end.

That is astonishing.

The church on earth looks weak.

It has no army.\
It possesses no empire.\
It does not control world history.

Yet God lets the suffering church see:

You are not merely spectators.

Your prayers are included in the work of God's kingdom.

We must not say that human prayer drives or controls God. God remains
the Lord of history.

The wonder is that the sovereign God chooses to include the prayers of
His people among the means through which He carries out His purposes.

So our prayers can become larger:

"Thy kingdom come."\
"Thy will be done in earth, as it is in heaven."\
"Let the gospel reach the nations."\
"Strengthen the persecuted church."\
"Revive Your church."\
"Let justice be revealed."\
"Bring the lost to Christ."

This is the prayer of the church.

## One Picture: A Censer of Rising Incense

Brothers and sisters, do not try to remember too many things today.

Remember one picture:

**a censer of incense.**

It rises slowly.

Perhaps you still cannot see the answer.

Your child has not returned.\
Your family member has not believed.\
The illness has not left.\
The relationship is not fully restored.\
The world remains confused.\
Justice still seems delayed.

But the incense is rising.

That means your prayer has not disappeared.

It has come before God.

When heaven seems silent, look at the incense.

When you want to stop praying, look at the incense.

When your prayer seems too small to matter, look at the incense.

Then tell yourself:

**Heaven may be silent for a time, but God never stops listening.**

## Conclusion: Between "How Long?" and "God Shall Wipe Away All Tears"

Now connect Revelation 6--8.

In chapter 6, the saints ask:

"How long?"

That is our present reality. We still wait.

In chapter 7, God shows the final outcome:

the Lamb shepherds His people, and God wipes away every tear.

That is our hope.

What should the church do between "How long?" and "God shall wipe away
all tears"?

Revelation 8 answers:

**Pray.**

Keep praying.

But do not pray only for yourself.

Pray for your family.\
Pray for the church.\
Pray for people who do not know Christ.\
Pray for your city.\
Pray for suffering churches.\
Pray for this generation.\
Pray for the gospel to reach the nations.\
Pray for God's kingdom to come.

Prayer is not escaping history.

Prayer is participating in God's work in history.

The world may imagine that presidents, kings, armies, financial markets,
and powerful institutions determine history.

Revelation lets the suffering church see another reality.

Behind earthly headlines stands a heavenly throne.

Above earthly powers reigns the Lamb who was slain and rose again.

And before that throne are:

**the prayers of all the saints.**

So if you have prayed for a long time---keep praying.

If you still do not see an answer---keep praying.

If your voice feels small---keep praying.

If heaven seems completely silent---keep praying.

Because heaven may be silent for a time, but God never stops listening.

The prayers that appear to disappear into the air are rising like
incense before the throne.

Bring that person you have carried in your heart before the throne
again.

Bring your pain before the throne again.

Bring our church before the throne again.

Bring this generation before the throne again.

And pray:

> "Thy kingdom come. Thy will be done in earth, as it is in heaven."
> (Matt. 6:10, KJV)

One day the trumpets will sound. Christ will triumph. Justice will be
revealed. God's kingdom will come in fullness.

And we will discover that not one prayer faithfully offered before Him
was forgotten.
