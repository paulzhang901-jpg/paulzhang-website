# All Creation Will Worship --- When the Lamb Receives Glory, Idols Lose Their Place

**Scripture: Revelation 5:11--14 (KJV)**

## Introduction: From Dead Silence to a Cosmic Chorus

Brothers and sisters, Revelation 5 does not begin with singing. It
begins with a heartbreaking question.

A mighty angel cries out, "Who is worthy to open the book, and to loose
the seals thereof?" (Rev. 5:2, KJV).

It is a question that echoes across the universe. Who can govern
history? Who can judge evil? Who can bring this broken world toward the
conclusion God has promised?

Yet Scripture says that no one in heaven, on earth, or under the earth
is able to open the scroll. John begins to weep.

Imagine the scene. The angel cries out, yet the universe has no answer.
No human being is worthy. No hero can save. No empire can solve the
problem. No earthly power can bring sin and death to an end.

It is a terrifying silence---the silence of humanity reaching the end of
itself, the silence of a world with no savior.

But while John is weeping, one of the elders says, "Weep not: behold,
the Lion of the tribe of Juda, the Root of David, hath prevailed"
(Rev. 5:5, KJV).

John turns, and instead of seeing a lion conquering the world through
violence, he sees "a Lamb as it had been slain" standing in the midst of
the throne.

Then the Lamb takes the scroll.

From that moment, the universe is silent no longer.

First the four living creatures and the twenty-four elders sing. Then
countless angels raise their voices in praise. Finally every creature in
heaven, on earth, under the earth, and in the sea joins the worship.

From "no one is worthy" to "the Lamb is worthy of everything."\
From John weeping to all creation singing.\
From cosmic silence to cosmic triumph.

This is the breathtaking vision of Revelation 5:11--14.

Today we will consider three movements:

1.  Heaven's triumphant praise: the slain Lamb is worthy of everything.
2.  Creation's ultimate worship: history will finally belong to the
    Lamb.
3.  Our earthly "Amen": surrender the hidden idols that still resist His
    rule.

## I. Heaven's Triumphant Praise: The Slain Lamb Is Worthy of Everything (5:11--12)

John says:

> "And I beheld, and I heard the voice of many angels round about the
> throne and the beasts and the elders: and the number of them was ten
> thousand times ten thousand, and thousands of thousands; saying with a
> loud voice, Worthy is the Lamb that was slain to receive power, and
> riches, and wisdom, and strength, and honour, and glory, and
> blessing." (Rev. 5:11--12, KJV)

Notice the contrast.

Earlier, in Revelation 5:2, one mighty angel asks with a loud voice,
"Who is worthy?" The whole universe is silent because no one is worthy.

Now, in verse 12, countless angels declare with a loud voice:

**"Worthy is the Lamb that was slain."**

Before, there was silence because no one could answer.\
Now, praise cannot be restrained.\
Before, we saw human inability.\
Now, we see the Lamb's victory.\
Before, John was weeping.\
Now, the angels are praising.

Brothers and sisters, this is not an ordinary concert. The whole
universe is declaring that history finally has its true center. Evil
will face judgment. Those who suffer have hope. The scroll has One who
is worthy to open it.

### 1. Why Is the "Slain Lamb" Worthy?

The angels do not say, "The most powerful Lamb is worthy."\
They do not say, "The most successful Lamb is worthy."\
They do not say, "The most influential Lamb is worthy."

They say:

**"Worthy is the Lamb that was slain."**

Here the gospel overturns the world's definition of glory.

The world says that whoever possesses power deserves glory.\
The world says that whoever wins the competition deserves respect.\
The world says that whoever has wealth, status, and influence is
successful.

Heaven says that the slain Lamb is worthy.

Why? Jesus did not conquer by taking the lives of others. He conquered
by giving His own life. He did not overcome by crushing sinners, but by
dying for sinners. He did not escape the cross in order to enter glory;
He passed through the cross into glory.

Philippians 2 says that Christ "humbled himself, and became obedient
unto death, even the death of the cross," and therefore "God also hath
highly exalted him" (Phil. 2:8--9, KJV).

The cross is not Christ's defeat. The cross is the way of His victory.

The Lamb was slain, yet He stands. He died, yet He lives. He gave His
life, yet He reigns over history.

### 2. Sevenfold Praise: Every False King Is Exposed

The angels ascribe seven things to the Lamb:

**power, riches, wisdom, strength, honour, glory, and blessing.**

Seven frequently communicates fullness in Revelation. The picture is one
of complete, unrestricted honor belonging to the Lamb.

In the Roman imperial world, power, honor, glory, wealth, and security
were readily associated with imperial rule and the claims surrounding
Caesar. Revelation pulls back the curtain and directs ultimate worship
elsewhere.

Heaven does not say, "Caesar is worthy."

Heaven says:

**"Worthy is the Lamb that was slain."**

Caesar's power passes away.\
Imperial wealth collapses.\
Military strength fades.\
Human glory disappears.\
But the kingdom of the Lamb endures.

Brothers and sisters, the world still preaches rival gospels to us.

It tells us that money can give us security.\
It tells us that a house, savings, immigration status, or a position can
give us honor.\
It tells us that our children's achievements can give us glory.\
It tells us that influence, followers, and applause can give us value.\
It tells us that if we can control enough, we will no longer be afraid.

Revelation says: none of these things is your savior.

Money is a useful tool, but it is not Lord.\
Work is a stewardship from God, but it is not Lord.\
Children are gifts from God, but they are not Lord.\
Ability is a gift from God, but it is not Lord.\
No nation, institution, technology, or market can become Lord.

Only the Lamb is worthy.

### Application: Lay Down the False Crown

Ask yourself:

What do I most desperately want to hold on to?\
What am I most afraid of losing?\
What have I made the source of my security, worth, and glory?

That thing may be the false crown in your heart.

The cross of the Lamb humbles us. Before Him, our abilities are no basis
for boasting. Our success cannot save us. Our glory does not ultimately
belong to us.

The Lamb invites us to lay down our false crowns and say:

"Lord, power belongs to You.\
Riches belong to You.\
Wisdom belongs to You.\
Glory belongs to You.\
I will no longer make myself king."

## II. Creation's Ultimate Worship: History Will Finally Belong to the Lamb (5:13)

John continues:

> "And every creature which is in heaven, and on the earth, and under
> the earth, and such as are in the sea, and all that are in them, heard
> I saying, Blessing, and honour, and glory, and power, be unto him that
> sitteth upon the throne, and unto the Lamb for ever and ever."
> (Rev. 5:13, KJV)

The circle of worship keeps expanding.

First the four living creatures and the twenty-four elders.\
Then countless angels.\
Now every creature.

Heaven, earth, under the earth, and the sea---this is comprehensive
language. No corner of creation lies outside God's claim, and no realm
can finally escape the sovereignty of the Lamb.

It recalls Psalm 148, where angels, sun and moon, mountains and trees,
animals and birds, kings and peoples are all summoned to praise the
Lord. It also recalls Philippians 2, which says that at the name of
Jesus every knee will bow, "of things in heaven, and things in earth,
and things under the earth" (Phil. 2:10, KJV).

### 1. This Is Not Fantasy; It Is History's Final Destination

Brothers and sisters, Revelation does not tell us to escape reality. It
shows us where reality is finally going.

Today we see confusion: war, hatred, division, moral disorder, broken
families, and struggles for power. We ask, "Why is evil still so strong?
Why is there so much injustice? Where is the kingdom of God?"

Revelation 5 tells us:

**What you see today is not the final page.**

The final page of history is not darkness.\
The final page is not violence.\
The final page is not empire.\
The final page is not death.

The final page is creation worshiping the One seated on the throne and
the Lamb.

This is an anchor for a suffering church.

The Roman Empire once appeared nearly invincible. Yet the empire belongs
to history. Its Caesars, armies, coins, and slogans passed away. But the
Lamb worshiped by those marginalized and persecuted Christians is still
worshiped around the world.

Christians therefore do not have hope because the world is easy. We have
hope because even when the world is difficult, we know who holds its
final outcome.

### 2. Worship Declares the Coming Victory Ahead of Time

When we worship on Sunday, we are not hiding in a church building to
forget the world outside.

We are practicing the reality toward which history is moving.

When we sing, "The Lamb is worthy," we are declaring the final outcome
in advance.

We worship not to escape war, but to refuse to bow to war, fear, and
darkness. We worship not to deny hardship, but to confess that hardship
is not our final lord.

Worship enables us to stand in the storm.

When you face illness, family difficulty, or financial pressure, worship
does not say, "The problem is not real." Worship says, "The problem is
real, but the Lamb still reigns."

When a church faces limited numbers, limited resources, relocation
challenges, or an uncertain future, worship is not self-comfort. Worship
confesses that the future of the church does not rest on human ability,
but in the hands of the Lamb.

### Application: Do Not Let the News or Your Circumstances Decide Whom You Worship

Many people hear the world's voice first every day and then allow it to
determine the condition of their hearts.

One news story produces fear.\
One movement in the market produces sleeplessness.\
One sentence from another person produces anger.\
One disappointing grade from a child feels like the collapse of life.\
One difficulty in the church makes everything seem hopeless.

Revelation invites us to hear heaven's voice first.

In the morning, do not first allow the news or your phone to tell you
who rules the world. Let the Word of God tell you first:

**The One seated on the throne and the Lamb reign.**

When you are afraid, read Revelation 5:13 and pray:

"Lord, I do not understand everything I see today, but I know that
history's final outcome belongs to You. I refuse to make fear my god. I
will worship the One seated on the throne and the Lamb."

## III. A Real "Amen" on Earth: Surrender the Idols That Still Resist His Rule (5:14)

The final verse says:

> "And the four beasts said, Amen. And the four and twenty elders fell
> down and worshipped him that liveth for ever and ever." (Rev. 5:14,
> KJV)

After the praise of countless angels and the chorus of all creation, the
passage ends with two simple responses:

The four living creatures say, **"Amen."**\
The twenty-four elders fall down and worship.

This is powerful.

The great chorus of heaven does not end merely with greater volume. It
ends with a concrete response: **Amen and bowing down.**

### 1. "Amen" Is More Than a Habit; It Is Yielding Our Rule to God

We often say "Amen" at the end of a prayer, but Amen is not merely a
closing word.

It expresses affirmation: this is true; I receive it; I agree.

When the four living creatures say "Amen," they affirm that the Lamb
truly is worthy, that glory and power truly belong to Him.

For us, saying Amen should therefore reach beyond our lips into our
obedience.

To say Amen is to say:

"Lord, You are the One who reigns.\
I will not keep the final authority for myself.\
Even the part of my life I least want to surrender belongs to You."

### 2. Bowing Down Means Taking Our Knees Back from False Gods

The elders fall down in worship.

To bow before the Lamb means recognizing Him as King. It also means
refusing to bow before rival kings.

Brothers and sisters, an idol does not have to be made of wood or stone.
An idol can be anything in the heart that we cannot release, cannot
imagine losing, and depend on as though our life rests upon it.

If on Sunday we sing, "The Lamb is worthy of everything," but from
Monday through Saturday we live in overwhelming fear because of stock
prices, housing values, our children's rankings, a supervisor's
expression, or other people's opinions, we need to ask honestly:

To whom am I really saying "Amen"?\
Before whom am I really bowing?\
Am I worshiping the Lamb, or am I worshiping my circumstances?

This does not mean Christians should stop caring about their children,
working hard, or planning their finances.

The issue is not whether you have responsibilities. The issue is whether
those responsibilities have become your lord.

You can work hard without allowing work to define your worth.\
You can love your children without allowing them to take God's place.\
You can make plans without believing that control is the same thing as
security.\
You can care about the world without allowing fear to steal your
worship.

### Application: Identify the Territory in Your Life That Is Still Rebelling Against His Rule

The Lamb does not ask only, "Did you attend church?"

He asks, "What territory in your life are you still unwilling to give to
Me?"

Perhaps it is money.\
Perhaps your children.\
Perhaps your marriage.\
Perhaps your temper.\
Perhaps a sin you refuse to release.\
Perhaps your plans for the future.\
Perhaps your insistence on control.

Today's "Amen" is not a casual word. It may become an act of spiritual
warfare.

You can pray:

"Lord, I confess that I have held this too tightly. I have acted as
though I cannot have peace without it. Today I give it to You. Even if
clouds remain tomorrow, I still confess that You are worthy to reign. I
say Amen to You."

## Conclusion: From the Cosmic Chorus to Your "Amen" Today

Brothers and sisters, Revelation 5:11--14 carries us through a
magnificent movement:

From the silence and tears of the first part of the chapter to the
triumphant praise of countless angels.\
From heavenly worship to the ultimate submission of all creation.\
From the cosmic chorus to your own "Amen" today.

The Lamb is worthy of everything.

He is worthy of power, riches, wisdom, strength, honor, glory, and
blessing.\
He is worthy of your Sunday and your Monday.\
He is worthy of your songs and your decisions.\
He is worthy of your emotions and your obedience.\
He is worthy of your future and the part of your life you most resist
surrendering.

May we no longer bow before money, success, reputation, fear, or
control.

May we take our knees back from every false god.

May we truthfully say every day:

> "Blessing, and honour, and glory, and power, be unto him that sitteth
> upon the throne, and unto the Lamb for ever and ever." (Rev. 5:13,
> KJV)

Amen.

## Closing Prayer

Lord Jesus, Lamb who was slain and who lives again, we confess that You
are worthy.

You are worthy of all power, riches, wisdom, strength, honor, glory, and
blessing.

Forgive us for so often placing money, achievement, children,
reputation, control, and security in the place that belongs to You.

Lord, today we lay down the false crowns of our lives. We take our knees
back from every false god. Help us not only to say with our lips on
Sunday, "You are worthy," but also to say "Amen" to You through the
choices we make every day.

When we face uncertainty, pressure, and fear, remind us that history's
final outcome is not in the hands of darkness, but in the hands of the
One seated on the throne and the Lamb.

May our families, our church, our work, and our future belong to You.

May all blessing, honor, glory, and power belong to the One seated on
the throne and to the Lamb for ever and ever.

In the name of Jesus Christ our Lord we pray. Amen.
