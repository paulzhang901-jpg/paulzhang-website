## Introduction

Young David played the harp for Saul, and Saul found relief and felt better. But this is not merely a story about music improving someone’s mood.

At a deeper level, this passage shows us a striking contrast: as the Spirit of the LORD came powerfully upon David, Saul experienced the Spirit of the LORD departing from him and was troubled by an evil spirit.

This reveals God’s rejection of Saul because of his disobedience. It also marks a significant step in the gradual transfer of Israel’s kingship from Saul to David. God began preparing David—not only by having him tend sheep in the wilderness, but also by using a seemingly ordinary gift to bring him into the palace.

When David entered the palace, he was not yet king.

He was just a young man.

What he held was not a crown, but a harp.

Yet God was already preparing his path.

So today, let us consider three things through this young harpist:

First, see people’s needs.  
Second, see the witness of a person’s life.  
Third, see God’s preparation.

## I. Saul’s Need

### 1. The Spirit of God departs

Scripture says:

> “The Spirit of the LORD departed from Saul.”

This is a deeply sobering spiritual picture.

Saul had experienced God’s choosing and the movement of God’s Spirit. Yet he repeatedly disobeyed God’s word and insisted on his own way. Finally, Samuel told him:

> “Because thou hast rejected the word of the LORD, he hath also rejected thee from being king.” (See 1 Samuel 15:23, 26.)

This does not mean that God suddenly abandoned Saul for no reason. It was the consequence of a long pattern of disobedience.

In the Old Testament, the Holy Spirit’s coming upon certain people often related to a particular office and the power God gave for it. In the New Testament, the Spirit’s indwelling of those who belong to Christ is an important promise of new-covenant salvation. We should therefore not mechanically apply Saul’s experience to every New Testament believer.

But Saul’s failure still gives us a very serious warning:

Do not treat God’s word lightly.

Do not enjoy God’s grace while persistently refusing God’s will.

Saul’s problem was not that he had never experienced God. He had experienced God, yet became increasingly determined to have his own way.

The Spirit of God departed from Saul but came powerfully upon David.

From this point on, their paths grew farther and farther apart.

Saul became increasingly bound by jealousy, fear, and anger.

David, meanwhile, was being shaped step by step in God’s hands.

### 2. Tormented by an evil spirit

Scripture says:

> “An evil spirit from the LORD troubled him.”

The expression translated as “demon” in the Chinese text is more precisely “evil spirit” or “harmful spirit.”

“From the LORD” does not mean that God himself is the source of evil. It emphasizes that even evil spirits are not outside God’s sovereignty. Job chapter 1 likewise shows us that Satan’s activity is limited by God.

So this passage is not offering us an “exorcism technique” to imitate. It reveals the sobering condition of Saul, who was already under God’s judgment.

His servants saw his suffering and suggested finding someone skilled at playing the harp.

Saul had a throne, but no peace.

He had power, but no rest.

He had people serving him, but they could not solve the deepest troubles of his heart.

That was Saul’s need.

A person can possess a great deal and still lose their inner peace.

So when we look at someone’s needs, we must look beyond what they have on the outside.

Some people look strong but are exhausted inside.

Some are successful but deeply lonely.

Some seem to have everything, yet have no peace in their hearts.

Saul needed someone to enter his world.

And God prepared David.

## II. Someone Else’s Recommendation—David’s Life Showing Through

The passage is fascinating.

David did not run to the palace gates and say:

“I’m gifted. Put me to work.”

Nor did he write a résumé and hand it to Saul.

Someone else recommended him.

First Samuel 16:18 says:

> “Behold, I have seen a son of Jesse the Bethlehemite, that is cunning in playing, and a mighty valiant man, and a man of war, and prudent in matters, and a comely person, and the LORD is with him.”

This verse is like a “life résumé” for David.

Someone had noticed him.

A genuine life often needs no self-promotion.

Others will see it.

### 1. Skilled at playing the harp

David was a skilled harpist.

He later became a very important figure in Israel’s tradition of song. Many biblical psalms are associated with David.

But the most important question here is not:

Does music itself have some automatic power to drive out evil spirits?

The passage does not turn David’s harp into a spiritual technique. Nor does it teach that playing a particular kind of music will always make evil spirits leave.

The key is this:

David was God’s anointed, and the LORD was with him.

The harp was a tool in his hands.

The real power was not in the harp itself, but in God.

The same is true today.

Some people play an instrument.

Some sing.

Some preach.

Some cook.

Some manage things.

Some run businesses.

Some fix things.

A gift does not have to be spectacular.

What matters is this:

Is your gift being used by God?

When David played the harp in the wilderness, he may never have imagined that it would one day bring him into the palace.

So do not look down on what God has placed in your hands today.

Use it faithfully. God knows when to put it to work.

### 2. A brave warrior

The servant also described David as “a mighty valiant man, and a man of war.”

Later, chapter 17 records David telling Saul that he had struck down a lion and a bear while tending sheep.

Where did those experiences happen?

Not in the palace.

Not on the battlefield.

But in the wilderness.

There was no applause.

There was no audience.

Only sheep.

Yet God was training him in that unnoticed place.

Brothers and sisters, we often think:

“When an opportunity comes, then I’ll get serious.”

“When someone notices me, then I’ll be faithful.”

But David was not like that.

He was already taking his work seriously when no one was watching.

So when the opportunity came, someone could say:

“I’ve seen this man.”

Real preparation often happens before the opportunity arrives.

### 3. Wise in speech

The servant also said that David was “prudent in matters.”

A person’s maturity shows not only in what they do, but also in what they say.

There are plenty of gifted people.

There are plenty of capable people.

But someone who can control their tongue and knows when to speak and how to speak is much harder to find.

Proverbs says:

> “A man hath joy by the answer of his mouth: and a word spoken in due season, how good is it!” (Proverbs 15:23)

When God prepares someone for his use, he trains not only their hands, but also their mouth.

Those who serve God especially need to learn:

Not to wound people carelessly.

Not to boast carelessly.

Not to judge carelessly.

Not to let emotions control their words.

David was not perfect. He had many failures later in life.

But here we see that even while he was young, David was already showing a measure of maturity that others could recognize in everyday life.

### 4. Handsome in appearance

The passage also describes David as “a comely person.”

Yet in this very chapter, God had just told Samuel:

> “The LORD seeth not as man seeth; for man looketh on the outward appearance, but the LORD looketh on the heart.” (See 1 Samuel 16:7.)

So we must never read this to mean:

“God used David because he was good-looking.”

It is precisely the opposite.

God looked first at his heart.

David’s appearance is simply part of the narrative’s description. It is not the foundation of God’s choice.

This reminds us, too:

Of course we can be neat, presentable, and respectful of others. But we must not make outward appearance the measure of a person’s worth.

People can see the outside.

God can see the heart.

What really determines whether someone can be used by God over the long haul is not the packaging on the outside, but the person within.

### 5. God’s presence

Finally comes the most important statement:

> “The LORD is with him.”

None of the earlier qualities matters as much as this.

Playing the harp well is good.

Being brave is good.

Speaking wisely is good.

Even good looks can be a gift of grace.

But without God’s presence, none of these can serve as the true foundation of a godly leader.

“The LORD is with him” points first to David’s real relationship with God.

At the same time, that relationship gradually became a testimony others could see in his life.

Brothers and sisters, other people may not know how long you pray each day.

They may not know how much of the Bible you have read.

But if you truly walk with God over time, certain things will gradually become visible to others.

Your temper will change.

The way you speak will change.

Your attitude toward difficulties will change.

Your patience with people will change.

Your view of money, power, and success will change.

Genuine spiritual life will eventually show through.

So David was recommended not simply because he could play the harp.

More importantly:

People could see that God was with him.

## III. God’s Preparation—A Harp Brings a Young Man into the Palace

Now let us look at the most remarkable part of this whole passage.

Saul needed a harpist.

His servant recommended David.

Jesse sent David into the palace.

David began serving before Saul.

From a human perspective, it was simply a job opportunity.

From God’s perspective, it was preparation.

### 1. God often uses a gift you already have to open the next door

David did not enter the palace by first proving he could be king.

He simply knew how to play the harp.

A harp became the doorway through which God brought him into the palace.

Brothers and sisters, we often ask:

“How will God use me in the future?”

But sometimes God first asks:

“What do you have in your hands right now?”

Moses held a staff.

A boy had five loaves and two fish.

David held a harp.

God often starts with something a person already has, has already practiced, and is already using faithfully.

Do not keep thinking that only impressive gifts have value.

If you can cook, you can serve people.

If you can drive, you can give people rides.

If you can listen, you can walk alongside someone.

If you can teach children, you can influence the next generation.

If you can run a business, you can bear witness to Christ at work.

If you have musical gifts, you can comfort people and lead them in worship.

The key question is not:

“How great is my gift?”

It is:

“Am I willing to place it in God’s hands?”

### 2. God’s preparation often begins before we understand

When David entered the palace, he did not yet know what would happen.

He did not know he would face Goliath.

He did not know Saul would later grow jealous and hunt him down.

He did not know he would become a fugitive.

Nor did he know that one day he would sit on Israel’s throne.

But God knew.

So God brought him into the palace first.

He let him see how a king governed.

How power worked.

He let him see Saul’s successes and also Saul’s failures.

All of this was preparation.

Often, we do not understand what we are going through today.

We ask:

“Why has God placed me here?”

“Why this job?”

“Why did I have to meet this person?”

“Why do I have to go through this season?”

Perhaps you do not know today.

But do not be too quick to say:

“This has no meaning.”

God often turns today’s experiences into tomorrow’s preparation.

### 3. Real preparation means learning to serve before learning to rule

Scripture says:

> “David came to Saul, and stood before him.”

Notice this:

God had already anointed David through Samuel.

But after being anointed, he did not immediately take the throne.

First he stood ready.

First he served.

First he waited.

First he learned.

This matters deeply for us.

Many people today like having a position but dislike preparation.

They like being seen but dislike being hidden.

They like leading others but are unwilling to serve others first.

But God’s way of training David was not:

I anoint you, and immediately give you a crown.

It was:

I anoint you, and then let you keep tending sheep.

Then I bring you into the palace to play the harp.

Then I let you face a giant.

Then I take you through the wilderness.

Only then do I entrust you with the throne.

God cares about more than whether someone can reach a certain position.

He cares even more about this:

What kind of person will you have become by the time you get there?

So, brothers and sisters, do not belittle the service you are doing today.

Do not belittle today’s small things.

Do not belittle the faithfulness no one else sees.

God may be preparing you.

## Conclusion

Brothers and sisters, today we have seen a young harpist.

His name was David.

He had already been anointed.

The Spirit of God had come powerfully upon him.

But there was still no crown in his hands.

Only a harp.

He was not sitting on a throne.

He was simply standing before Saul, serving him.

Yet God had already begun to work.

Saul had a need.

Others had a recommendation.

David had a gift.

God had a plan.

When these things came together, a door opened.

So today, let us remember three things:

First, see people’s needs.

Do not look only at someone’s outward identity, position, and success.

Saul was a king, yet he still needed help.

Second, let your life become a testimony others can see.

David did not praise himself.

Someone else said:

I have seen him.

He plays the harp well.

He is brave.

He speaks wisely.

More importantly:

The LORD is with him.

Third, trust that God is preparing you.

The “harp” you hold today may look very ordinary.

But you do not know how God will use it tomorrow.

So do not rush after a position.

Be faithful first.

Do not rush to be noticed.

Prepare yourself first.

Do not think your work is meaningless simply because it is small right now.

God knows how to bring the sound of a harp from the wilderness into a palace.

## A Final Call to Respond

Brothers and sisters, I want you to ask yourself three questions today:

First, is there someone near me who is suffering and needs help?

Do not look only at the outside.

Ask God to help you see the need.

Second, if someone were introducing me, what would they say?

Would they say:

This person is gifted.

This person is dependable.

This person speaks with care.

This person is loving.

And most importantly—

God is with this person.

Third, what do I have in my hands today?

Perhaps not a crown.

Just “a harp.”

A job.

A family.

A small act of service.

A place no one else has noticed.

Please do not look down on it.

Give it to God.

Use it faithfully.

Because you do not know:

God may be using the harp in your hands today

to prepare the path you will walk tomorrow.

May we all become people like this:

Gifted, but not proud.

Capable, but willing to serve.

Chosen by God, but willing to wait.

Faithful even when no one is watching.

Until one day, people can look at our lives and say:

> “The LORD is with him.”
