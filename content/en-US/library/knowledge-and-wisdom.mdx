# The Difference Between Knowledge and Wisdom

**By Muchangke**

We live in an age that values knowledge more and more, yet seems to
speak less and less about wisdom.

We want our children to attend the best schools, earn the highest
degrees, and study the most sought-after fields. We hope they understand
technology, finance, management, and artificial intelligence. Ideally,
we want them to be capable in almost everything.

As a result, degrees become more advanced, résumés more impressive, and
minds more crowded with information.

But there is one question we rarely stop to ask seriously:

**Does having knowledge necessarily mean having wisdom?**

Several cases I have come across recently have caused me to think more
deeply about this question.

## 1. A 5,000-Yuan Prize Revealed Something Worth Far More Than 5,000 Yuan

Not long ago, an incident at Shanghai Jiao Tong University attracted
public attention, even though at first glance it did not seem
particularly serious.

Two students entered a competition as a team. One student was mainly
responsible for the core technical work, while another student, surnamed
Fan, handled matters such as registration and presentation. The team
eventually won a prize of 5,000 yuan.

The problem began with the distribution of that money.

According to information later released by the university, Fan engaged
in dishonest conduct during the distribution of the prize money,
including falsifying a payment record and concealing the actual amount
of the award. After the matter came to light, the university imposed
disciplinary action.

A young person capable of gaining admission to Shanghai Jiao Tong
University is certainly not lacking in knowledge.

He knew how to register for a competition, how to participate, how to
prepare materials, and even how to use modern technology to produce a
record that might appear convincing enough to escape scrutiny.

In one sense, all of these are forms of "knowledge."

But knowing how to do something skillfully is not the same as knowing
whether it should be done at all.

That is one of the most fundamental differences between knowledge and
wisdom.

Knowledge asks, **"Can I do this?"**

Wisdom asks, **"Should I do this?"**

Knowledge is concerned with methods; wisdom is concerned with direction.

Knowledge can help a person move quickly. Wisdom determines where that
person is actually going.

If the direction is wrong, the faster you travel, the farther you may
end up from your destination.

## 2. 2,200 Yuan and 28 Yuan: Money Is Not What Is Truly Expensive

Two other stories have also circulated online.

One story tells of a young person who had just completed a master's
degree and secured a teaching position. He reportedly sold a gaming
account for 2,200 yuan, then later used the account-recovery mechanism
to take it back. What may have seemed like "just a little more than two
thousand yuan" eventually cost him far more because of legal and
credibility issues.

Another online story is even more sobering.

It is said that a well-educated young white-collar worker with a decent
income received a food delivery worth only a few dozen yuan, then
complained that the delivery rider had failed to deliver the order and
obtained a refund from the platform.

The specific details of these online stories may not all have been fully
verified through authoritative sources, so there is no need for us to
rush to judgment about the individuals involved.

But the question raised by these stories is very real:

**If someone is willing to sell his integrity for 2,200 yuan---or even
28 yuan---then what is truly expensive?**

The real issue has never been 5,000 yuan, 2,200 yuan, or 28 yuan.

The real issue is this:

**When personal gain is placed in front of me, is there a line in my
heart that I refuse to cross?**

If there is no such line, today it may be 28 yuan. Tomorrow it may be
2,800. One day it may be 280,000.

Character rarely collapses all at once in some dramatic,
earth-shattering moment.

More often, it is formed---or eroded---through one small and seemingly
insignificant choice after another.

Today, I take a small advantage.

Tomorrow, I tell a small lie.

The next day, I tell myself, "No one will know anyway."

Later, I reassure myself, "Everybody does it."

Over time, a person does not suddenly lose integrity. He sells it little
by little through countless small decisions.

That is why Jesus says in Luke 16:10:

> "He that is faithful in that which is least is faithful also in much:
> and he that is unjust in the least is unjust also in much."

The Bible understands human nature remarkably well.

Big things are often simply small things magnified.

The kind of person you are when 28 yuan is at stake may very well be the
kind of person you remain when 2.8 million yuan is at stake.

## 3. Knowledge Is Knowing About Things; Wisdom Is Knowing How to Live

So what exactly is knowledge, and what is wisdom?

Simply put, knowledge is our understanding of things.

If you understand physics, you possess knowledge of physics.

If you understand medicine, you possess medical knowledge.

If you understand law, you possess legal knowledge.

If you understand computers, you possess technical knowledge.

To a considerable degree, an academic degree demonstrates that a person
has received systematic training in a particular field of knowledge.

We should therefore respect knowledge, and we should certainly encourage
our children to study diligently.

The Christian faith has never been opposed to knowledge.

The problem is this:

**Knowledge is not wisdom.**

A person may hold a doctorate and still not know how to treat others.

A person may know the law inside and out while constantly searching for
loopholes.

A person may study the complexities of human nature and yet fail to deal
honestly with the people closest to him.

A person may even be clever enough to design an elaborate deception, yet
lack the wisdom to realize that he is destroying something far more
valuable than money.

Wisdom does not merely answer the question, "What is this?"

Wisdom asks:

**How should I live?**

How should I treat money?

How should I respond to personal gain?

How should I treat people who are weaker than I am?

How should I handle relationships?

What should I choose when no one is watching?

If lying could benefit me, should I still tell the truth?

If taking advantage of someone brings no immediate punishment, will I
still hold to my principles?

A degree may not answer these questions.

Yet these are the questions that ultimately shape the kind of person we
become.

## 4. Why Does Wisdom Begin with "Fear"?

Proverbs 9:10 says:

> "The fear of the LORD is the beginning of wisdom: and the knowledge of
> the holy is understanding."

This is profoundly important.

The Bible does not say, "Having more knowledge is the beginning of
wisdom."

It does not say, "A higher degree is the beginning of wisdom."

It does not even say, "Intelligence is the beginning of wisdom."

It says:

**The fear of the LORD is the beginning of wisdom.**

Why?

Because true wisdom begins not by resolving the relationship between a
person and knowledge, but the relationship between a person and God.

Why would someone choose honesty when no one is watching?

Why refuse to take advantage of an opportunity when it would be easy to
do so?

Why refuse to bully, exploit, or trample on someone weaker?

Because that person knows:

**People may not see, but God sees.**

That is what it means to fear God.

The fear of God does not mean living in terror that God is waiting for
an opportunity to punish us. It means recognizing that above my desires
there is God; above my interests there is truth; and above my personal
choices there remains a standard of right and wrong.

I am not the final judge of my own life.

Therefore, I cannot say, "I want it, so I have the right to take it."

I cannot say, "No one knows, so it does not matter."

I cannot say, "Everyone else does it, so it must be right."

To fear God is to know that even where human eyes cannot see, there is
still a line I must not cross.

## 5. Our Relationship with God Flows into Our Relationships with Others

This is also why the Bible binds love for God and love for others so
closely together.

When our relationship with God is rightly ordered, it gradually
transforms the way we relate to other people.

A person who truly fears God learns to be honest because God is true.

He learns to keep his word because God is faithful.

He learns mercy because God has shown mercy to us.

He learns self-denial because Christ first gave Himself for us.

He learns not to covet what belongs to others because he understands
that the true value of life is not measured by how much one possesses.

Conversely, when reverence for God disappears, intelligence can easily
turn into calculation, ability into a tool of self-interest, and
knowledge into a means of satisfying desire.

That is why greater intelligence can sometimes make a person more
dangerous rather than less.

If character cannot govern ability, then greater ability may simply
produce greater damage.

Knowledge is like the engine of a car.

Wisdom is like the steering wheel and the brakes.

There is nothing to celebrate about an engine becoming more and more
powerful if the car has neither steering nor brakes.

## 6. What Are We Really Trying to Give Our Children?

Parents today may need to reconsider a very important question:

**What kind of people are we actually trying to raise our children to
become?**

Of course we hope they will attend good universities.

Of course we want them to have knowledge, competence, professional
skills, and a stable income.

There is nothing wrong with any of that.

But if a child can solve the most complicated mathematics problem yet
does not understand honesty;

if he can enter the best university yet does not know the fear of God;

if he can earn a great deal of money yet does not know that some money
should never be earned;

if he knows how to defeat others but not how to love them;

then something essential is still missing from our education.

I increasingly believe that every person has at least four accounts in
life:

**a knowledge account, an ability account, a credibility account, and a
character account.**

The first two may help you open a door.

The last two determine whether people will want you to remain inside.

A degree may help someone obtain a first job,

but credibility determines whether others will continue entrusting
responsibility to that person.

Intelligence may help someone seize an opportunity,

but character determines whether that opportunity will eventually be
destroyed by his own hands.

So I want to say something to our children and to every young person:

**My child, I do not require you to become someone who stands above
everyone else. But I hope you will become someone others can trust.**

You may never have a dazzling academic record.

You may never hold an enviable position.

You may even live an ordinary life from beginning to end.

But do not lose your integrity for 5,000 yuan.

Do not cross a moral boundary for 2,200 yuan.

And do not let your conscience become cheaper and cheaper for the sake
of 28 yuan.

There are things in this world that, once lost, cannot be bought back at
any price.

They include your credibility, your conscience, your character, and the
trust others place in you.

## Conclusion: The Diploma That Really Matters

We live in an age of exploding knowledge.

Artificial intelligence can now give us, within seconds, information
that once might have taken a person years to acquire.

The children of the future will almost certainly know more than we do.

Yet what will truly be scarce in the future may not be knowledge at all.

It may be wisdom.

Knowledge teaches us how the world works.

Wisdom teaches us how we ought to live in that world.

Knowledge can be gained from books.

Skills can be developed through training.

Degrees can be earned through examinations.

But the Bible tells us:

> "The fear of the LORD is the beginning of wisdom."

True education, therefore, is not merely about putting knowledge into
the mind. It is also about putting reverence for God into the heart.

When a person truly fears God, he begins to understand what may be done
and what must not be done; what may be taken and what must never be
taken; when he must remain faithful even if no one is watching; and when
he must refuse to sell his conscience even if doing so costs him
something.

Five thousand yuan is not much.

Two thousand two hundred yuan is not a fortune.

Twenty-eight yuan is almost insignificant.

And yet it is precisely in such "small things" that a person writes
down, choice by choice, who he really is.

A diploma is the certificate a school gives you.

Character is the certificate life gives you.

And true wisdom is not about how much you know,

how much you possess,

or even how intelligent you are.

True wisdom is this: when the world tells you, "As long as you do not
get caught, it is fine," there is still a voice within you that says:

**No. I cannot do this.**

**Because I fear God.**

And because I know---

Some advantages must never be taken, no matter how tempting.\
Some lines must never be crossed, no matter the cost.\
And some things, once lost, cannot be recovered by even the highest
degree in the world.
